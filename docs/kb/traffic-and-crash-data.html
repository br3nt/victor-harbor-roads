Traffic and Crash Data

What public data says about how traffic moves today (R8, R3). Full write-up: Stream D: traffic data, crashes, road ownership and approvals (Victor Harbor).

The through road

Victor Harbor's main through road is one State road (Department for Infrastructure and Transport [DIT] road 04760): Victor Harbor Road → Adelaide Road → Hindmarsh Road → Torrens Street, ending at the Torrens/Victoria Street roundabout [S-D05].

Trend: Hindmarsh Rd went from 14,000 (count year 2009) to 18,400 (2021) to 19,300 (2024) — about 38% over 15 years. Torrens St has been flat (15,600 → 15,100 → 15,500) [S-D02]. Inference: growth is landing on Hindmarsh Rd (the approach), not in the town-centre stretch — consistent with new housing and shopping at McCracken / north.

Turning counts — the best local data

  • 12-hour (7am–7pm) turning counts at seven Torrens St / Hindmarsh Rd intersections on Wed 26 Apr 2017, in 15-minute bins with light/heavy/cycle splits [S-D19 App. A]. Taken during school holidays; described as reasonably typical.
  • About 7,600 vehicles entered and 6,700 left the town centre in those 12 hours.
  • Peaks are midday, not commuter: the busiest hour at each intersection falls between 11:15 and 14:15, carrying 1,281–1,823 vehicles/hour; Seaview/Eyre is the busiest (1,823, 11:45–12:45).
  • Torrens St right turn into Ocean St about 700 vehicles/day; Stuart St about 530 left and 180 right turns.

These counts are nine years old and from April, not peak summer. They are the calibration starting point (R26); fresh counts would be better.

The State counts are the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) annual average daily traffic figures [S-D01]. A movement between two roads takes the geometric mean of the two road factors; no adjustment is made for term time against school holidays, because no local evidence exists for one (an assumption).

Validation: compared with the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) 2024 annual average daily traffic, five of six projected positions on Torrens St and Hindmarsh Rd are within ±10% (ratios 0.91 to 1.09), and all six are within 17% (lowest 0.83, at Coral St, cause not established). Against the consultant's estimated daily volumes [S-B04 p.22], the projection agrees for Coral St (1.01) and Seaview Rd (0.87) but not for Eyre Terrace (2.17) or Torrens St (1.68; the consultant's 8,500 contradicts the State's own count of about 15,500 for that street). The comparison is rough: the State figure is an all-days annual mean, the 2017 count was a school-holiday weekday, and the 12-hour to daily conversion carries 5–10% uncertainty Projecting the 2017 turning counts to 2025. The model carries about 0.80 of the projected 2025 counted volume at matched movements (figure reported by the simulation stream).

Inference: a 2018 council count on Armstrong Rd of about 6,000 vehicles a day [S-B34] against the consultant's later estimate of 7,200 [S-B04 p.22] would be a 20% rise, faster than population. It compares a count with an estimate, so it is only a hint that the 1.17 factor for council streets may be low Projecting the 2017 turning counts to 2025.

Summer peak evidence

  • Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT): more than 11,000 vehicles/day on Victor Harbor Rd north of town in the summer holiday peak [S-D18], against 7,700–8,400 annual average — roughly +30–40% (inference).
  • Parking surveys: the core central business district (CBD) (Ocean St between Torrens and Coral St, Coral St between Torrens St and Flinders Pde) was consistently more than 80% occupied on a typical weekday (Wed 2 Dec 2015) as well as on 30 Dec 2015, when All streets within the CBD including the length of Flinders Parade were at or near capacity [S-D21 s7.2]. What summer changed was the extent: on a typical day parking was readily available outside the core. (The 26–29% average in the 2017 strategy is across all ~2,000 on-street spaces in the wider study area, not the core.)
  • The 2015–16 study warned the Hindmarsh/Seaview/Eyre roundabout would reach capacity in summer peaks if the Grantley Avenue crossing stayed open [S-D21 s13.3.15].
  • No hourly, permanent-counter or seasonal profiles exist on the State data portal.

Crashes 2021–2025 (town centre box)

97 crashes: 65 property damage only, 24 minor injury, 7 serious injury, 1 fatal (a pedestrian, January 2023, near Torrens St / Stuart St). 40 right-angle crashes, 7 involving pedestrians. By year: 22, 21, 12, 24, 18 [S-D13] [S-D16].

Hotspots: Torrens St / Eyre Tce roundabout 6 (5 right-angle); Acraman St / Hill St 6; Acraman St / Seaview Rd 5; Armstrong Rd / Crozier Rd 4. Wider area: 354 crashes, 5 fatal. Street labels are approximate (crash data has coordinates only, snapped to the nearest street).

Inference: the crash record points at right-angle crashes at priority junctions on the side streets (Acraman, Armstrong, Crozier) more than at the arterial roundabouts the signals proposal targets.

Disagreements between sources

  • Daily volumes: SMEC's Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) report gives Hindmarsh Rd 9,500 and Torrens St 8,500 — roughly 1.5–2 times lower than the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT)'s figures (see Due Diligence Assessment) [S-B04 p.22].
  • Torrens St speed limit: Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT)'s legal-limit layer says 50 km/h [S-D05]; OpenStreetMap (OSM) tags 60 km/h [S-E01]. DIT notes its layer may not match posted signs. Check on the ground.
