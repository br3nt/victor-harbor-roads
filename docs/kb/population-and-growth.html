Population and Growth

How many people live here now, how many the council plans for, and where they will live (R6). Full write-up: Stream C: Population, dwellings, growth areas, holiday and seasonal demand.

Now

  • Census 2021: 16,139 usual residents [S-C01]. Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Estimated Resident Population 30 June 2025: 17,457 (+1.9% on 2024) [S-C03].
  • Median age 60 (South Australia [SA] 41); about 42% aged 65 or over [S-C01]. Inference: a retiree-heavy population fits the midday (not commuter) traffic peak in the 2017 counts (D12).

The two growth scenarios to simulate

The council's own growth strategy (Urban Growth Management Strategy [UGMS] 2050, Sep 2025) gives two scenarios over 25 years [S-B10 pp.13, 30, 41]:

ScenarioExtra people by ~2050Extra dwellingsRateApprox. population
Moderate (historic trend)≈ 8,000≈ 4,600up to 185 dwellings/yr≈ 25,000–25,500
High (State's Greater Adelaide Regional Plan, GARP)> 19,200≈ 12,030401 dwellings/yr (Greater Adelaide Regional Plan [GARP] targets 318 → 410 → 475 per year by decade)≈ 36,000–37,000

Actual building since 2021 has averaged 159 dwellings a year [S-B10 p.20] — below even the moderate rate. The Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS) itself says the Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) targets exceed realised and forecast demand and that faster growth cannot occur without an investment in structure planning and infrastructure planning and delivery [S-B10 p.21].

Inference (reconciles the headline): 12,030 dwellings × 76% occupied × 2.1 people ≈ 19,200 people, matching the Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS) high figure. So the Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) claim that the population will approximately double to about 36,000 by 2051 [S-A03] is the high scenario; the fact sheet does not mention the moderate one. SMEC's report does present both (between 9 thousand (if historic trends persist) and 20 thousand) and explains it models the Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) high case as an "ultimate" scenario without a specific year because of a legislative requirement for consistent planning [S-B04 pp.43, 45, 49-50].

The 25,292 puzzle

  • The council's 2041 figure of 25,292 equals the State's high series for the Local Government Area (LGA), 25,291.5 [S-M02] [S-C25]. The State's medium series for 2041 is 23,641, about 1,650 lower [S-M02].
  • The "additional 13,500 dwellings" attached to that figure in the council's text [S-C25] does not appear in the State's population files [S-M02], so the pairing is not confirmed. Inference: from the 2021 base of 16,428 to 25,292 is about 8,860 more people; 13,500 dwellings at 76% occupied and 2.1 people each (the factors used above) would house about 21,500. The two numbers do not describe the same growth, so 13,500 is probably a land-supply or capacity figure rather than the dwellings implied by the projection. Land-supply detail from the Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) is still needed to check this.

Is the population tracking the State series?

Inference (our arithmetic, not a forecast). The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Estimated Resident Population (ERP) for 30 June 2025 was 17,457 and has grown about 1.95% a year (compound, 2018 to 2025: 15,248 to 17,457) [S-C03]. One more year at that rate gives 17,457 × 1.0195 ≈ 17,797 for 2026. The State's medium series for 2026 is 18,408 and its high series 18,685 [S-M02], so the Estimated Resident Population (ERP) trend is about 610 people (3%) below medium and about 890 (5%) below high. Population is therefore tracking below even the State's medium series for 2026. The medium series implies about 2.3% a year from 2021 to 2026 (16,428 to 18,408), faster than the recent 1.95%. If 1.95% a year simply continued to 2041, the result would be about 23,800 (17,457 × 1.019516), close to the State's medium figure of 23,641 for that year, far below the 25,292 the council quotes. That extrapolation ignores any acceleration from new housing.
