His free-text ideas, condensed

  • Victor is no longer a country town; council approves city-style density, so single lanes won't cope. 30km/h roads increase congestion, not improve it. Invest in four-lane roads.
  • Suspects no simulations were run; asks why no alternatives or supporting data are offered.
  • If speed limits are lowered, make them timed and zoned to special events, shown on e-ink variable signs rather than long time-of-day rule plates.
  • Widen the current ring route to four lanes (least environmental impact, big throughput gain, serves existing needs); then improve the side/tributary roads off it.
  • The roundabout at the main entry into Victor should be a two-lane roundabout.
  • Option 1 (new outer ring road) is expensive, far from the action, will be little used, while load on the existing ring route keeps rising.
  • Land use: blocks are shrinking each decade, too little room for trees and third places; consider commercial/retail zones closer to Encounter Bay.

Facebook comments (21 Nov 2026)

On a post in the public "Victor Harbor & Port Elliot, Middleton, Goolwa, Hindmarsh Island Community" Facebook group [S-F01], whose many comments are a sign of public sentiment about the loop; Brent's comments captured in screenshots [S-F02] [S-F03].

  • Has anyone built simulations to validate the changes? Feasible today.
  • 30 km/h is a mistake; maybe some zones reduce speed at certain hours, using e-ink signs showing the current limit. Slowing traffic increases congestion.
  • Plan ahead to widen roads to double lanes; development density now matches Adelaide; one-way streets are a mistake.
  • Traffic lights maybe, but improve roundabouts instead, e.g. slip lanes around the corners so two cars can pass through in a direction at once.

A related claim raised in the public discussion, worth testing: every car, truck and caravan would be routed around past the foreshore and Warland Reserve, past the family recreation reserve, before reaching the caravan park or shops [S-F03]. See R23.
