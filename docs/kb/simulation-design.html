Simulation Design

How we will answer the plan's simulation requirements (R13, R14, R15, R16, R23, R26). This is a draft; the toolchain check and data availability from the research sweep will firm it up.

Engine

Eclipse Simulation of Urban MObility (SUMO) (open-source microscopic traffic simulator): imports OpenStreetMap, models roundabouts, signals, lane changes, speed limits and per-vehicle routes, and logs per-trip travel times and per-lane/per-movement delays. Every scenario is a SUMO network variant plus a demand variant, scripted so results regenerate from the data.

Network (R12)

  • OpenStreetMap (OSM) extract of greater Victor Harbor and its approach roads, cleaned: lanes, speeds, roundabout geometry, signal plans, turn restrictions checked against aerial imagery.
  • Scenario networks: one per option in the Options Register (one-way loop, signals, 30 km/h, shared zones, ring-road variants, Brent's options, new options).

Demand

  • Residents: synthetic households placed by Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) mesh-block dwelling and person counts, minus unoccupied dwellings; trips by purpose (shopping, dining, services, gym, school, work, recreation) and hour, from published trip-generation rates; destinations chosen among real POIs by distance and attractiveness.
  • Visitors: a separate layer sized to the summer anchor (>11,000 vehicles/day on Victor Harbor Rd) since holiday homes alone explain only ≈ +29% (Housing and Visitors). holiday-home occupancy (unoccupied dwellings filling up in season), short-term rentals, caravan-park guests, and day-trippers entering on Victor Harbor Rd / Port Elliot Rd scaled to seasonal counts.
  • Through and freight traffic: from state-road volumes and heavy-vehicle shares.
  • Future years: two scenarios from the council's Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS) — moderate (≈ +8,000 people by 2050, 185 dwellings/yr) and high (Greater Adelaide Regional Plan [GARP], ≈ +19,200, 318→410→475 dwellings/yr) — run at 2031, 2041 and 2051, with new households placed in the northern growth areas and infill (Population and Growth). Method: scale and relocate households to the council's forecast years and growth areas (north growth area, infill), keeping assumptions explicit.

Conditions

School-term weekday off-peak · weekday midday peak (the town centre peaks 11:15–14:15, not at commuter times — D12) · weekday AM and PM · Saturday midday · summer-holiday peak day · event day with published road closures (New Year's Eve, Schoolies — see Seasons and Events). Each condition runs with several random seeds so results are distributions, not single numbers.

Calibration (R26)

Match simulated link volumes to observed counts (target GEH statistic < 5 on most counted links) and simulated travel times to observed ones (Google/TomTom-style travel-time samples if obtainable). Where no observation exists, mark the result as assumption-driven and run sensitivity tests.

Outputs

  • Accessibility (R14): for each residential area × point of interest (POI) type × condition × scenario × year: travel-time distribution with median, mean, p95, p99 and a histogram. Travel times are usually right-skewed, so we report the observed shape rather than assume a normal distribution.
  • Intersections (R15): for each intersection × approach × turning movement: mean and p95 delay (wait to turn), queue length, throughput, by condition and scenario.
  • Safety proxy: conflicting movements and right-angle exposure at priority junctions, since right-angle crashes dominate the crash record (D11).
  • Network: total vehicle-hours, km travelled, heavy vehicles and caravans past the foreshore / Warland Reserve (R23a), effect of speed limits on throughput (R23b).
  • Results pages in this knowledge base, with data embedded in <script type="application/json"> and interactive charts / animated replays.
