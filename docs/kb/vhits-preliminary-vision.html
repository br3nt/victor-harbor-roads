Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) Preliminary Vision

What the City of Victor Harbor is consulting on: a preliminary Vision for the Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS), not a draft strategy. Covers R1, R25, R27. Consulted 29 Oct – 5pm Sun 22 Nov 2026 [S-A01].

What exists and what doesn't

  • The only published document is an 8-page fact sheet (PDF created 28 Oct 2026, no author or consultant named) [S-A03].
  • No traffic counts, modelling, costs, staging, street-by-street loop description or consultant report is published [S-A01].
  • How the Vision was prepared, per the fact sheet: reviewed the Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS), analysed demographic and transport data, reviewed feedback from previous consultations, met state and regional agencies and transport associations. None of that data is published [S-A03 p.1].
  • After consultation: results posted as a report on YourSay; the final Strategy adopted by Council "over the coming months"; no meeting date given [S-A01].

Stated objectives (R25)

  • Long-term strategy to "guide Council investment, decisions and advocacy" to state and federal governments [S-A03 p.1].
  • Population forecast to double to approximately 36,000 people by 2051; the town will need to move over double the number of people [S-A03 p.1, 3]. Baseline year and figure not stated.
  • Town centre: A pedestrian friendly town centre that is easy and safe to get around, reducing congestion and making parking easier [S-A03 p.4].
  • Ring road: move freight around the city safely and efficiently while enabling easy east–west travel [S-A03 p.5].

Ring Road element (R27)

  • C5 Option 1, new outer ring road: moves freight around the town away from more residential areas, with high speed; the existing ring road becomes a 60 km/h "link road" with better connections between the existing south and the future north community. Route on the map runs from the south-west near Tabernacle Road, up the Greenhills Road side past Day Road, around the west of the growth area to Hutchinson Road, then east across the top [S-A03 p.5].
  • C6 Option 2, retain current ring road: acknowledged to create a barrier between south and north communities; needs a new activity centre north of it for the north community's services and retail [S-A03 p.5].
  • No cost, staging, freight volumes or rationale beyond those bullets. The current ring road is not named in the text (inference: the Adelaide Road / Waterport Road arterial).

Other elements (out of scope, noted for completeness)

Public transport: internal bus routes, Victor–Goolwa passenger service (train or O-Bahn), park and ride, intermodal hub at the station, external buses, transfer stops. Walking and cycling: Encounter Bikeway, priority footpaths, links to the north community, mountain bike park [S-A03 p.6–7].
