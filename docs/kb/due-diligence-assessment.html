Due Diligence Assessment

How well the evidence supports the proposals (R3). Brent suspected the data was rudimentary; this is what the record shows. Main document: SMEC Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) Phase 1 Report.

Direct answers

QuestionAnswer from the record
Was the one-way loop, 30 km/h or the signal plan modelled?No. None is in the model scenarios (only a "Do Nothing" network was run), and no SIDRA Intersection or microsimulation work is mentioned. SMEC's text has no one-way loop or 30 km/h figure; it raises signals at the Torrens/Seaview and Torrens/Crozier junctions and lower central business district (CBD) speeds or shared zones as options to consider, without testing them [S-B04 pp.50, 52, 70-71]. Testing is scheduled for Feb–Jun 2027 [S-B04] [S-B03 p.78].
Was the outer ring road studied or costed?No route study, cost estimate or Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) position found. SMEC says a northern freight bypass should be explored [S-B04 pp.66, 74-75] (see Ring Road History).
Were new traffic counts taken?No Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) count programme is documented. SMEC's AADTs are "estimated" and undated; it says there is no count information on the main east-west road corridor outside of the extremities [S-B04 pp.21-22, 72].
Were holiday peaks considered?Only descriptively, from one permanent counter on Victor Harbor Rd (2025). The model represents a typical weekday [S-B04 pp.41, 45].
Is there a calibrated model?Victor Harbor Roads Assessment Model (VHRAM) is a simplified transport demand model, base year 2021, one GARP-high "ultimate" scenario, "Do Nothing" network only. It is said to be calibrated, but no calibration statistics, count sites or validation criteria are published [S-B04 pp.43-49, 78].
Did SMEC flag limits?Yes: no testing of alternative growth scenarios has been completed; count gaps limit certainty; not sufficient time to review network and land-use assumptions with Council [S-B04 pp.77-78].
Parking evidence?2015–16 surveys, reused on the assumption that similar patterns still apply [S-B04 p.37].
Peer review?None mentioned [S-B03] [S-B04].
Were options compared?No options appraisal for the town-centre elements (loop vs two-way, 30 vs 40 km/h, signals vs roundabouts). The maps were presented as designed to initiate engagement and discussion [S-B03 p.78].
Time for councillors to scrutinise?Three open briefings: 11 May (60 min), 15 Jul (60 min) and 12 Oct (30 min), 14 days before approval on 26 Oct [S-B11] [S-B12] [S-B13]. No briefing notes are published. The engagement plan scheduled the fact sheet and survey to be uploaded to Your Say by 23 Oct, with public launch on 29 Oct after the vote [S-B03 p.87].

What SMEC's model did say about the town centre

  • Torrens St and Hindmarsh Rd would be nearing capacity but not exceeding in the ultimate scenario [S-B04 p.54].
  • "Signalisation of intersections" suggested mainly as safer pedestrian crossing points; Consideration of signalising roundabouts and the installation of traffic signals … will be required as key intersections along the primary east-west corridor reach capacity [S-B04 pp.50, 71]. It names Torrens St's critical junctions with Seaview Road and Crozier Road and expects Seaview/Eyre to need an upgrade [S-B04 pp.52, 54].
  • SMEC recommended new peak and non-peak counts, parking turnover surveys with seasonality, and more regular State counts on Armstrong Road, Torrens Street and "Victoria Road" (as SMEC writes it) [S-B04 p.72].

A data conflict to resolve

SMEC's Table 3 gives Hindmarsh Rd 9,500, Torrens St 8,500 and Victoria St 9,300 vehicles/day [S-B04 p.22]. Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT)'s published AADTs are roughly 1.5 to 2 times higher: Hindmarsh Rd 19,300 / 17,300, Torrens St 15,500 / 12,900 (count year 2024) [S-D01]. SMEC's footnote on the Hindmarsh Rd figure reads Volume running into Town Centre southwest of Hindmarsh Bridge, which may mean inbound only; Torrens St and Victoria St carry no such note. Possible explanations: one direction only, different sections, or an error. If Table 3 is what feeds the Victor Harbor Roads Assessment Model (VHRAM) (not shown), the model would understate congestion. SMEC's own definitions put an "Arterial" road at in excess of 15,000 vehicles per day [S-B04 p.79], yet Table 3 classes Hindmarsh Rd and Torrens St as Arterial at 9,500 and 8,500. Ask Council which figures the model uses.

Budget

Phase 1 cost not disclosed. Phase 2: $100,000 under contract C0000243, paid from "Project #Planning (100087)" (the same number is labelled "C&D Admin" elsewhere); the 2026/27 Annual Business Plan says $120,000. No capital provision for any proposal in the asset plan or long-term financial plan [S-B03 p.79] [S-B17 p.41] [S-B18] [S-B20 p.28].

Assessment

Inference (ours): no published analysis tests the specific choices put to the public. The one technical report has no one-way loop or 30 km/h figure; it raises signals, lower central business district (CBD) speeds and a northern freight bypass only as options to consider, says its own data and time were limited, and the testing that could support or reject any of them is scheduled after the community has been asked to rate them. That is the core of Brent's point that options should be simulated before they are consulted on.
