The survey then labels these "Option 1" and "Option 2: Retain current Ring Road" (existing road "converted to a 'link road' with 60 km/h")S-B03 pp.91-94, S-A05

Links to growth

The growth areas lie north of Armstrong Rd: a priority growth area, deferred urban (up to 2,400 allotments) and future greenfield (about 3,600) [S-B04 pp.46-48]. A structure plan for the priority growth area (about $200,000) is a Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS) action; ring-road interface and intersections are flagged for it [S-B06 p.320] [S-B08 p.92].

Gaps

No documented reason why the outer route moved from "should be explored" (SMEC, Aug 2026) to part of the Vision (Oct 2026); no route study, corridor width, cost or Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT)/National Heavy Vehicle Regulator (NHVR) advice on an outer route. Brent's alternative — widen the existing ring road to four lanes (B3) — is one reading of the "duplication" item in the January 2026 working-group notes, but those notes don't say what duplication means or who intends it.
