Decision Timeline

How the current proposals came about, in date order (R2, R4). Council decisions link to their resolution notes, which link to the people who moved, seconded and voted (People and Roles). Full detail: research/B-provenance.md.

The short version

  1. The council's last adopted town-centre position (the 2017 strategy) kept roundabouts and two-way streets, left signals at Seaview/Eyre open pending modelling, and proposed no loop and no 30 km/h. From 2002 to 2025 council documents treated the existing ring road as the freight route (2024 B-double gazettal, 2025 Urban Growth Management Strategy [UGMS]). Earlier plans did float one-way streets (2006) and "circulation loops" (2014).
  2. A councillor's motion in December 2025 started a traffic investigation. SMEC's Phase 1 report (June–August 2026) contains no one-way loop and no 30 km/h figure. It raises signals at the Torrens St junctions with Seaview and Crozier Roads, lower speeds or shared zones in the central business district (CBD), and a northern freight bypass as options to consider or explore, and names the existing ring road the preferred freight route. It tested none of these.
  3. Councillors had three open briefings (11 May 60 min, 15 Jul 60 min, 12 Oct 30 min). The draft consultation maps, branded SMEC and City of Victor Harbor, were tabled on 26 October 2026; in Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) documents they are where the one-way loop and a 30 km/h town centre first appear, and they make the outer freight ring road part of the "Vision". The record does not show who originated each element. Council approved consultation that night; no division was called. The minutes do not record debate, but the council's video shows about five minutes of it, in which no speaker mentioned the loop or the 30 km/h limit [S-M05].
  4. Testing, modelling and costing of these ideas is scheduled for February–June 2027 — after the consultation, and after Council is due to endorse an "Ultimate Vision" in February 2027.

What the record doesn't show

  • Who originated the one-way loop and the 30 km/h town centre, which are not in SMEC's report but are on the SMEC- and council-branded maps. Briefing presentations and the 30 Sep staff workshop notes are not published.
  • Why the outer freight ring road moved from "should be explored" in SMEC's report to part of the "Vision".
  • What councillors understood the loop, the 30 km/h town centre and the outer ring road to be when they approved consultation. They did debate item 10.1 for about five minutes, but, per the auto-captions, none of the speakers mentioned those elements [S-M05]; the briefings (11 May, 15 Jul, 12 Oct) are not recorded in public.
