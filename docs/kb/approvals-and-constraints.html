Approvals and Constraints

Who has to agree before the council's proposals can happen, and the rules they apply (R11, E19). This may decide whether some proposals are feasible at all.

Who owns which road

  • State (Department for Infrastructure and Transport [DIT]): Adelaide Rd, George Main Rd, Hindmarsh Rd, Hindmarsh Tiers Rd, Inman Valley Rd, Port Elliot Rd, Torrens St, Victor Harbor Rd, Victoria St — the council's own list [S-D17], matching DIT's layer [S-D05].
  • Council (confirmed by the City of Victor Harbor's Public Road Register, 3 December 2021): the register lists each of these as "Council" in its "Maintained by" column: Acraman St (p.2), Albert Pl (p.2), Bay Rd (p.3), Coral St (p.6), Crozier Rd (p.6), Esplanade (p.8), Eyre Tce (p.8), Flinders Pde (p.9), Ocean St (p.17), Seaview Rd (p.21), Stuart St (p.22), Torrens Lane (p.24) and Waterport Rd (p.24) [S-M01 pp.2-24]. The register also lists Torrens Lane East and Torrens Lane West and the Victor Harbor Ring Road among roads maintained by Council [S-M01 p.25].
  • State, as the same register shows: it lists Torrens St (p.24), Victoria St (p.24), Hindmarsh Rd (p.12) and Adelaide Rd (p.2) as maintained by the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) [S-M01 pp.2, 12, 24], agreeing with the list above.
  • One discrepancy (not resolved): the register lists Hindmarsh Tiers Rd as maintained by Council [S-M01 p.12], whereas the council's own list in the first bullet gives it as a State road. The register is dated December 2021 and the other list is undated here; the road is outside the town centre and does not affect the loop.
  • Both proposed signal sites, and much of the one-way loop, are on State roads (Torrens St, Hindmarsh Rd, Victoria St).

Freight and strategic route status

Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT)'s functional hierarchy classes Victor Harbor Rd, Adelaide Rd, Hindmarsh Rd and Torrens St as both Primary Freight Routes and Strategic Routes, and as Direct Tourist Routes [S-D08].

Speed limits

  • The primary source is the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) Speed Limit Guideline for South Australia, version 3, 13 October 2023 (extracts only: sections 4.1, 4.3, 5.7, 5.7.1 and 5.16). It supersedes the secondary summary S-D22 (another council's agenda), which we no longer rely on for the points below.
  • Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) approves speed limits on all roads: the Department is responsible for approving speed limits on all roads and road-related areas in South Australia. The only exception is that councils may install school zones, koala crossings and short sections of 40 km/h speed limit in conjunction with wombat crossings [S-M04 s4.1]. A town-centre limit on council streets is therefore still a DIT decision.
  • The guideline does allow 30 km/h: A 30 km/h or 40 km/h speed limit may be applied to pedestrian activity areas including shopping precincts, town centres, local residential areas, retirement villages, beachfront esplanades and holiday house / shack areas. Which of the two suits depends on factors such as road width and length, separation of pedestrians and vehicles, physical calming and the proportions of pedestrian and vehicle traffic [S-M04 s5.7].
  • For "high pedestrian activity centres" the guideline sets conditions: the limit is not intended for streets with short holiday or tourism peaks; roads identified as freight routes or major traffic routes are unsuitable for high pedestrian activity centre speed limits; roads with lanes wider than 4 m are generally unsuitable without narrowing or other calming; and kerb extensions or pedestrian refuges should be investigated as an alternative or addition [S-M04 s5.7.1].
  • On 30 km/h specifically: it may be adopted for high pedestrian activity centres where pedestrian volumes are very high and is best suited to locations where the road has been specifically designed to create a speed environment of 30 km/h and alternative routes are available to drivers to discourage through traffic. The examples given are Hindley Street and Gresham Street in Adelaide [S-M04 s5.7.1]. A 40 km/h limit may be suited to roads where high pedestrian and vehicle movements are generated consistently throughout the day over the year [S-M04 s5.7.1].
  • Variable speed limit signs: the guideline points to Austroads guidance (Guide to Traffic Management Part 9, 2020, and Guide to Smart Motorways, 2016) and says school-zone variable signs are not used in South Australia [S-M04 s5.16]. The extract we hold does not say whether time-of-day or event-based variable limits would be approved for a town centre.
  • Consultation on a limit change is the road authority's job, and for proposals with broad impact the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) may work with the council; the extract lists the stakeholders to involve [S-M04 s4.3].
  • From the secondary summary only (not checked in the primary extracts): area limits need the mean speed of the streets to be under 10 km/h above the proposed limit, and consultation results go to the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) with the application [S-D22 p.126].
  • Precedent: Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) would not support lower limits on freight-route roads through Strathalbyn town centre [S-D22].
  • A 30 km/h limit for the Esplanade / Flinders Pde (Albert Pl to Coral St) was reported in Feb 2025 as council-supported (Victor Harbor Times headline; secondary, not archived, unverified — do not cite until checked against council minutes).

Inference (from the primary guideline): 30 km/h is permitted in principle, so the question is whether a 30 km/h limit in "all areas of the town centre" meets the guideline's tests. Three look hard on the text alone: (1) Torrens St and Hindmarsh Rd are classed as primary freight and strategic routes [S-D08], and the guideline calls such routes unsuitable for high-pedestrian-activity limits [S-M04 s5.7.1]; (2) the guideline says the limit is not intended for streets with short holiday or tourism peaks, and whether Victor Harbor's pedestrian activity is on a consistent daily basis throughout the year is something we hold no pedestrian counts to show; (3) 30 km/h is best suited where the road is designed for it and alternative routes exist. The proposal may therefore be most achievable on council streets inside the block, with calming works, where the register shows the council maintains the roads [S-M01] — but the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) would still have to approve the limit [S-M04 s4.1]. Whether it would do so is unknown; the question for council is in Questions for Council.

Traffic signals and one-way streets

  • The Minister's instrument delegating traffic-control-device powers lets councils install devices on roads in their care and control, with listed exceptions [S-D23] (Barossa copy, 2021; Victor Harbor's own not found). Inference: signals on Torrens St / Hindmarsh Rd are a Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) decision.
  • The 2015–16 council study said Torrens/Victoria improvements are not the responsibility of Council as both roads are Department of Planning, Transport and Infrastructure (DPTI)'s [S-D21 s13.3.11].
  • Council made Almond Avenue one-way by public notice under s.32 of the Road Traffic Act 1961 [S-A07e] — the route for council streets.

Prior expert advice on the signal sites

  • Torrens/Seaview/Eyre (a proposed 2026 signal site): signals may be warranted in the long term but would need three lanes per approach and land acquisition; they were not justified at that time, and If additional land is released and developed … the traffic signals may be required [S-D21 s13.3.14]. The 2017 strategy left them open: further detailed investigations and modelling will be required [S-A08a p.14].
  • Torrens/Crozier (a proposed 2026 signal site): the 2017 strategy recommended a roundabout, since built [S-A08a p.13].
  • Torrens/Victoria (not a proposed 2026 signal site, but on the loop): the roundabout performs well for most times of the day and peak seasons; signals will most likely increase delays [S-D21 s13.3.11].
