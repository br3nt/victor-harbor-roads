S9 — The council's ring-road options (VHITS Option 1 vs Option 2)

Stream S9 (22 Nov 2026). Brent's question: "what of the ring route options?" The October 2026 consultation (S-A03 p.5, S-B03 pp.91–94, S-A05) offered two:

  • Option 1 — new outer ring road (C5): a new high-speed road moving freight round the town, away from residential areas; the existing ring road (Welch Rd / Armstrong Rd) becomes a 60 km/h "link road" with better connections between the existing south and the growing north.
  • Option 2 — retain the current ring road (C6 in the options register): today's network. The consultation pairs it with a new activity centre north of the ring road (a demand change, not built here). Brent's four-lane ring road (B3) and our junction fixes (N7, roundabouts at Armstrong/Crozier, Armstrong/Greenhills and Hindmarsh/Grantley, from S7) are two ways of keeping and improving the current ring road.

Every year is stated: "2025" is the model's now (2025 demand data, the latest available in 2026); 2031 and 2041 are the S2 growth scenarios (UGMS moderate and high series).

1. How C5 was built

The council's ring-road map (S-A04b, S-B03 p.94) draws the outer road as an "indicative" dashed line. S7 tried to georeference it and failed: the labelled junctions gave scales nearly a factor of two apart (the map is schematic). So C5 is not traced from the drawing. It is built from the route the text and map describe — from Inman Valley Rd near Greenhills Rd, north along the west of the growth area to Hutchinson Rd, then east across Hindmarsh Tiers Rd to Victor Harbor Rd — using existing OpenStreetMap roads and road reserves wherever they follow that description, plus short new sections where none do. Two readings are built.

C5b keeps the outer road off the growth areas' Greenhills Rd frontage (driveways, future estate entries), which is how a freight bypass would normally be placed, and reaches Victor Harbor Rd by the shortest line "across Hindmarsh Tiers Rd" as the text says. C5 is the cheaper reading (0.4 km new road) and follows the map's line up Greenhills Rd. Both are inferences; neither is a design.

S9-03. Cross-section and junctions

  • Value: one lane each way, 100 km/h, on new and upgraded sections; new sections are typed as rural secondary roads, so the A-INT-01 right-turn bays apply at their junctions like any arterial. The outer road has priority 11 (as Inman Valley Rd and Greenhills Rd). Where it meets or follows local roads (Greenhills Rd at the join and at Hutchinson Rd, Hindmarsh Tiers Rd at Hutchinson Rd; C5b also Stock / Spring / Glenvale / Cartwright Rds), the other legs are lowered to priority 10, so the outer road is the major road and side roads give way. At Inman Valley Rd (Swains Crossing junction) the outer road and Inman Valley Rd west form the main road (equal priority; netconvert picks the faster pair), so Inman Valley Rd traffic to and from the town gives way there, the layout of a freight route turning off at a T. At Victor Harbor Rd (trunk, priority 13) the outer road gives way. Plain give-way junctions throughout, no roundabouts.
  • Why: the consultation shows no junction designs. Give-way junctions are the cheapest reading and how the existing rural roads meet today; roundabouts at the two arterial junctions would be the natural upgrade if they queued. They do not (part 3).
  • Sensitivity: low for totals; the Victor Harbor Rd give-way decides how long right-turning outer-road traffic waits, which matters only if the outer road carries much more than the model gives it.

S9-04. The existing ring road as a 60 km/h link road

  • Value: every Welch Rd and Armstrong Rd edge at 60 km/h or less (14 edges, 8.1 directed km lowered from 80–100 km/h). Junctions unchanged.
  • Not modelled: "better connectivity to … future growing north communities" (new junctions on the link road). The S2 demand model loads the growth areas onto the existing rural roads (Waggon Rd, Day Rd, Greenhills Rd, Fuller Rd; S2 M3/M15) and has no estate streets, so any new connection points would be invented. A 60 km/h main road also makes gaps easier to accept from the side roads (SUMO's gap model uses the approaching speed), which is the part of "better connections" the model does capture.

S9-05. Trucks prefer the outer road

  • Value: the ring road's edges carry SUMO routingType="c5_link"; a routing-preference file (sim/data/networks/c5_bays.prefs.add.xml, loaded by duarouter and by sumo's en-route rerouting) gives trucks a priority of 0.5 on them, i.e. a truck counts the link road at twice its travel time when it chooses a route. Cars are unaffected. Trucks still drive the link road at the normal limit when their trip starts or ends near it.
  • Why: the council's text says the outer road "moves freight around Victor Harbor". A ban would strand trucks whose trip ends on the ring road (their trip ends are the nearest road, S2/A-INT-01), and a truck-only speed limit would physically slow them and the cars behind. A routing preference is how a "no through trucks" sign and a gazetted freight route work: through trucks go round, local trucks do not.
  • Check: a truck from Inman Valley Rd (west) to Victor Harbor Rd north or to Waterport Rd routes round the outer road in both C5 readings (test tests/scenarios/test_c5_ring.py); to Waterport Rd it takes the outer road although the link road would be about 6 km shorter.
  • Sensitivity: medium for truck numbers on the link road, low for everything else (trucks are about 3% of trips: 1,959 of 67,239 on a 2025 term weekday).

S9-06. What the model can and cannot say about Option 1

  • Through traffic between the gateways is small in this demand model: the 2025 term-weekday demand has 1,959 truck trips a day, and Hutchinson Rd carries almost nothing today (4 vehicles a day in the 2025 summer baseline, seed 1). The outer road's main users would be through freight (Inman Valley Rd ↔ Victor Harbor Rd north / Waterport Rd) and, from 2031, growth-area residents.
  • Free-flow routing in today's network already sends Inman Valley Rd ↔ Victor Harbor Rd traffic over Swains Crossing Rd and Greenhills Rd to Armstrong Rd, not along Inman Valley Rd to the Armstrong Rd roundabout; and Inman Valley Rd ↔ Port Elliot Rd traffic through the northern residential streets (Canterbury Rd, Galpin Ave, Pine Ave, Field Ave, The Parkway) to Hindmarsh Rd, not the ring road. That second route is a model artefact (no truck restrictions on local streets) that neither option changes.
  • No cost: the council gives none. Order of magnitude only (S7-04 classes): C5 is 9.9 km of rural arterial, almost all upgrading of council roads (sealing Water Reserve Rd, bringing Greenhills Rd and Hutchinson Rd to a 100 km/h freight standard, a 100 km/h crossing of the Inman River at Swains Crossing) plus 0.4 km new; C5b has 3 km of new road. Both are at least the top of the $$$ class ($3–10 M) and probably above it, against $$$ for N7's three roundabouts. A class label, not an estimate.

Where Option 1's delay comes from

Lowering Welch Rd / Armstrong Rd to 60 km/h makes the ring road no faster than going through town, so about half its traffic leaves it: some for the outer road (growth-area traffic heading north, through traffic), the rest onto Hindmarsh Rd, Adelaide Rd and the town-centre streets. The ring-road junction queues all but vanish (77.8 → 8.5 vehicle-hours at 2041 high growth, because there is less traffic and 60 km/h gaps are easier to accept), but the diverted traffic queues at the side-street and roundabout junctions in town: the largest increases at 2041 high growth are Bridge Tce at the Grantley Ave crossing (+17 vehicle-hours a day), Coral St / Torrens St (+10), Eyre Tce / McKinlay St (+5) and the Hindmarsh Rd side streets (Renown Ave, The Drive, The Parkway, Peace Ave, Wattle Dr, +2–4 each). Growth-area trips to and from the town centre take about 0.4–0.5 minutes longer (7.4 → 7.8–7.9 minutes), not shorter. The truck preference works on the ring road (trucks on it 411 → 29–38 a day) but trucks using town-centre streets rise (394 → about 505), because local deliveries that used the 100 km/h ring road now cut through town too.

C5s (the C5 outer road with the ring road left at today's limits) was built to separate the outer road from the 60 km/h downgrade; its runs did not finish in time (gaps).

4. Verdict: Option 1 vs Option 2 with targeted junction fixes

  • Option 1 as consulted (outer road + existing ring at 60 km/h) is worse than doing nothing in every run that finished: +18% (C5) and +30% (C5b) time stopped at 2041 high growth, +83% on the one 2025 summer seed. It succeeds at its stated aim (through trucks off the existing ring road, the ring junctions decongested) but moves local traffic and delay into the town centre and onto Hindmarsh Rd, the opposite of the town-centre objective. It is also by far the most expensive option (about 10 km of road to a 100 km/h freight standard).
  • Option 2 with targeted junction fixes (N7: roundabouts at Armstrong/Crozier and Armstrong/Greenhills, plus Hindmarsh/Grantley) is the best option tested at every growth level: −24% at 2041 high growth (3 seeds, ranges clear), −4 to −6% on 2031 weekdays and −37 to −43% on 2031 summer days (1 seed), −10% on a 2025 summer day. Widening the ring road (B3) without fixing its junctions makes things worse (+23% at 2041).
  • So, in this model: keep the ring road (Option 2), fix its junctions as growth arrives, and do not lower it to 60 km/h. Whether an outer road helps without downgrading the existing one is the open question C5s was built for.
  • Limits: the demand model has no estate streets or new links for the growth areas (S2 M3/M15), little through freight (about 3% of trips are trucks) and no "better connections" from the link road to the north (S9-04), all of which an Option 1 design would add; the outer road's alignment is our reading (S9-01/02); 2031 runs are single seeds; the C5 2025 summer result is one seed.

Sensitivity of the alignment

C5 (0.4 km new road, along Greenhills Rd) and C5b (3.0 km new road west of it, Mont Rosa Rd to Victor Harbor Rd) give the same picture: similar outer-road volumes (Hutchinson Rd about 3,000–3,400 a day), the same diversion into town; C5b is worse (+30% vs +18%); it leaves Greenhills Rd as a 50 km/h local road and its outer road is less direct for traffic from growth areas 1 and 3 beside Greenhills Rd (not separated further). The verdict does not depend on which reading is used; it depends on the 60 km/h downgrade (C5s, gap).
