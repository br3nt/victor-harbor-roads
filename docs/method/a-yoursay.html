Stream A: Council's YourSay consultation (VHITS) and related YourSay projects

Retrieved 21 Nov 2026 (Adelaide). Source ids refer to research/A-yoursay-sources.tsv; files in sources/A/.

1. Summary

  • Full name: "Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy" (VHITS = that name). It is a long-term strategy to "guide Council investment, decisions and advocacy to state and federal governments for decades" [S-A03 p.1]. The consultation is on a preliminary Vision for Victor Harbor's Future Transport Network, not a finished strategy [S-A01].
  • Consultation closes 5:00 pm, Sunday 22 November 2026 (all submissions, survey included, as stated) [S-A01; S-A03 p.8]. It opened Thursday 29 October 2026; a drop-in session ran 4:30-6:30 pm Wednesday 11 November 2026 at the Civic Centre foyer, 1 Bay Road [S-A01 Key Dates].
  • Four Vision Elements: Town Centre, Ring Road, Public Transport, Walking and Cycling [S-A03 p.3].
  • Town Centre (5 features): (1) 30 km/h in "all areas of the town centre"; (2) shared use zones in pedestrian hotspots, 10-20 km/h, pedestrians have priority; (3) one-way outer loop around the town centre; (4) traffic lights at the Torrens Street/Hindmarsh Road intersections with Seaview Road and Crozier Road; (5) digital parking signage showing live space counts [S-A03 p.4].
  • Ring Road (2 options): Option 1 new outer Ring Road with existing Ring Road becoming a 60 km/h "link road"; Option 2 retain the current Ring Road and add a new activity centre north of it [S-A03 p.5].
  • No costs, staging, traffic counts, modelling, exact street-by-street loop description, or consultant report is published on YourSay for VHITS. The only document in the library is the 8-page fact sheet [S-A01 Document Library]. The fact sheet gives no budget.
  • Population is "forecast to double to approximately 36,000 people by 2051" [S-A03 p.1]. Growth areas come from the UGMS 2050 (adopted 22 Sep 2025) and the State's Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) [S-A03 p.1-2; S-A07a].
  • The survey has 6 steps / 10 questions; the final step (About You) requires first name, last name, email, suburb, so capture stopped there. Nothing was submitted [S-A05].
  • Next steps: results to be posted as a report on the same page; the final Strategy will be posted once Council adopts it "over the coming months"; projects are "gradually triggered as the community grows" and "in line with availability of local, state and federal government funding" [S-A01; S-A03 p.8]. No adoption date is given.
  • Important lead: the 2017 Tonkin Consulting "Town Centre Traffic Movement and Car Parking Strategy" (Council-endorsed Oct 2017) recommended a roundabout (not signals) at Crozier Road/Torrens Street and explicitly kept the Torrens/Victoria Street roundabout [S-A08a p.13]. It also floated electronic parking-space signage [S-A08b s.5.4].

2. Findings by expected item

E1: the strategy itself

Status of document. There is no draft strategy document yet. The fact sheet says "We have developed a preliminary Vision" and "We want to know what our community thinks about the preliminary Vision ... before we complete the Strategy" [S-A03 p.1]. Author/consultant is not named on the fact sheet or page. (PDF metadata: Adobe InDesign, created 28 Oct 2026 [S-A03].)

How the vision was prepared [S-A03 p.1]: looked at the UGMS to see where growth is likely; analysed demographic and transport data; reviewed community and stakeholder feedback from previous consultations; met key state government and regional agencies and transport associations. None of that data is published in the fact sheet.

Project page text [S-A01]: "Victor Harbor is growing and Council is planning a transport network that works with this growth and makes it easy and safe for community, business, goods, and visitors to get around." The Strategy "will look at how our roads, public transport, parking, freight, walking and cycling paths can work together". Page sub-title: "Council is seeking feedback on a preliminary Vision for Victor Harbor's Future Transport Network - including the town centre, ring road, public transport and walking and cycling."

Background [S-A03 p.1-2]: population forecast to double to approx. 36,000 by 2051 (the page does not state the base year or baseline figure); GARP identifies Victor Harbor as "a key growth region" and hub of the Southern Fleurieu; "Victor Harbor is projected to need to move over double the number of people around" (p.3).

Town Centre element (Vision: "A pedestrian friendly town centre that is easy and safe to get around, with measures to reduce congestion and make parking easier") [S-A03 p.4; map S-A04c = sources/A/S-A04-survey-images/img-501.jpg]:

  1. Lower speed limits "in all areas of the town centre to 30 km/h". The map shades a single blue area: roughly the foreshore block east of Torrens Street, bounded by Seaview Road/Torrens Street to the north, the Flinders Parade/foreshore side to the east, and Victoria Street to the south. The same blue area is where the map places the digital parking sign, the pedestrian icon (shared use) and bus/bike icons. Exact boundary streets are not labelled on the map; the shading is the only definition. Inference: this approximates the Mainstreet/Ocean Street/Esplanade core, but the 30 km/h area is not a text-defined boundary.
  2. Shared use zones: "10 - 20 km/h zones where pedestrians have priority over vehicles", in "pedestrian hotspots". No specific streets or hours given in the text; map icon sits inside the blue area. No hours stated.
  3. One-way outer loop, "to reduce traffic congestion". The map (dashed arrows, S-A04c) shows a loop of travel arrows: up Torrens Street toward Seaview Road signals (arrows pointing north-east), then south down the eastern side (Flinders Parade side), west along the southern edge, then back out along Victoria Street heading west (arrow pointing west at the Victoria Street label), with another arrow set linking Victoria Street up to the Torrens/Crozier signals. Inference: loop direction cannot be read unambiguously from the picture and no street names beyond Torrens Street, Seaview Road, Crozier Road and Victoria Street are labelled; the loop sits on the outside of the blue 30 km/h/shared area. Text never names the loop streets. Needs a question to Council or the underlying design.
  4. Traffic lights at "Torrens Street/Hindmarsh Road intersections with Seaview Road and Crozier Road". The map shows two signal symbols: one at Torrens Street/Seaview Road (top) and one at Torrens Street/Crozier Road (lower left). Existing treatment per 2017 strategy: a roundabout at Torrens/Seaview/Hindmarsh (the Torrens/Seaview Roads roundabout [S-A08a p.6]) and an unbuilt proposed roundabout at Torrens/Crozier [S-A08a p.13]. Inference: this proposal would replace those arrangements with signals.
  5. Digital parking signage "to show live information about the number of spaces available in distinct locations" (no locations stated; one icon on map).

Ring Road element (Vision: "A well-located Ring Road that safely and efficiently moves freight around our city, whilst also enabling safe and easy east-west travel for residents and visitors") [S-A03 p.5; map img-500.jpg = S-A04b]: - Option 1, new outer Ring Road: "moves freight around Victor Harbor, away from more residential areas, with high speed". "Existing Ring Road converted to a 'link road' with 60 km/h speed limit and better connectivity to existing south and future growing north communities." Map: red dashed line starting at the south-west near Tabernacle Road/the existing ring road line, running north-west along the Greenhills Road side past Day Road, around the west of the growth area to Hutchinson Road, then to the north-east and east across the top of the growth area. Labelled roads: Hutchinson Road, Greenhills Road, Day Road, Waggon Road, Inman Valley Road, Waterport Road, Hindmarsh Road, Seaview Road, Crozier Road. - Option 2, retain current Ring Road: "creates a barrier between the existing south and future growing north communities"; "a new activity centre will be required north of the current Ring Road" for services and retail for the future north community. Map: blue line = current Ring Road (south-west from Tabernacle Road side, curving north-east to Waterport Road and east); activity-centre symbol north of it near Hindmarsh River/Waggon Road. - Both options retain the existing town centre. Route detail is limited to the map; the fact sheet gives no cost, staging, freight volumes, or rationale beyond the bullets above. The existing "Ring Road" is not named in the text (the map line follows the arterial linking Waterport Road with the south-west); Inference: it is the existing Adelaide Road/Waterport Road arterial. - Option 1 note: only the stated 60 km/h link-road speed is given; the speed of the new road is "high", unquantified.

Public Transport element [S-A03 p.6; map S-A04d img-502.jpg]: internal bus routes with better frequency; modern Victor Harbor-Goolwa passenger service (e.g. train or O'Bahn); park and ride north of town near Waterport/Hindmarsh Road; intermodal hub at Victor Harbor station in the town centre; external bus routes (e.g. Cape Jervis, Adelaide); key transfer stops (e.g. hospital).

Walking and Cycling element [S-A03 p.7; map S-A04a img-499.jpg]: Encounter Bikeway along the coast to Goolwa; completed priority footpath network (schools, shops); expanded walking and cycling network linking to the future north community; connection to Victor Harbor Mountain Bike Park.

E2: survey content (full walk-through; nothing submitted)

Title: "Survey: Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy" (status "Open"). Embedded on the project page (not an iframe), Social Pinpoint platform. Intro: "This survey asks for your feedback on each Element of the preliminary Vision ... the town centre, ring road, public transport, and walking and cycling." Progress bar: 0%, 17%, 33%, 50%, 67%, 83%. Full text in S-A05. Each Element step shows the same map image as the fact sheet (S-A04a-d). Scale for rating questions: Strongly Support / Support / Neutral/Unsure / Do not support / Strongly Do Not Support (Q3 Ring Road uses "Strongly do not support"). A "Clear selection" control is present. Rating questions are "Required"; free-text questions are optional.

  • Step 1 of 6, Town Centre. Q1 (required, matrix): "Do you support the following key features of the Vision Element for the town centre?" Rows: 30 km/h; shared use zones 10-20 km/h; one-way outer loop; traffic lights at Torrens Street/Hindmarsh Road intersections with Seaview Road and Crozier Road ("to improve traffic flow and make it easier and safer for pedestrians"); digital parking signage. Q2 (text, optional): "Do you have other ideas about making the town centre easy to get around?"
  • Step 2 of 6, Ring Road. Text restates "Two Options". Q3 (required, matrix): "To what extent do you support each option for the Ring Road?" rows Option 1 and Option 2 (each rated independently). Q4 (text): "Do you have other ideas about the Ring Road?"
  • Step 3 of 6, Public Transport. Q5 (required matrix, 6 rows: internal bus; Goolwa passenger service; park and ride; intermodal hub; external bus routes; key transfer stops). Q6 (text): "Do you have other ideas about improving public transport?"
  • Step 4 of 6, Walking and Cycling. Q7 (required matrix, 4 rows). Q8 (text): "Do you have other ideas about improving walking and cycling?"
  • Step 5 of 6, Other Comments. Q9 (text): "Do you have any other comments to inform the Integrated Transport Strategy?"
  • Step 6 of 6, About You (capture stopped here; Next is blocked by validation). Q10 (required, multi-select): "What is your connection to the Victor Harbor?" options: Resident, Rate payer, Worker, Business, Community or business group, Stakeholder, Visitor, Other (please specify). Required text fields: First Name, Last Name, Your primary email address, Suburb. Then a Submit button (not clicked).
  • Survey design note: the rating questions are pre-framed (each feature is described with its stated benefit, e.g. "to reduce traffic congestion", "to improve traffic flow and make it easier and safer for pedestrians"); there is no option to choose between features, ask about cost, ask about local business impact, or see alternatives. Inference (labelled as such): useful for the owner's submission/critique.
  • Hard-copy survey available at Civic Centre; written submissions by email localgov@victor.sa.gov.au (shown in fact sheet p.8), post PO Box 11, Victor Harbor SA 5211, or in person at the Civic Centre [S-A01; S-A03 p.8].

4. Discoveries

  • 2017 Council-endorsed strategy (Tonkin) rejected or deferred several of the now-proposed measures: kept the Torrens/Victoria Street roundabout ("installation of traffic signals will most likely increase delays in comparison to the roundabout") [S-A08a p.13]; preferred a roundabout at Torrens/Crozier (preliminary design existed; a 2016 Black Spot application was unsuccessful) [S-A08a p.13]; for Hindmarsh/Seaview/Eyre Terrace roundabout, community asked for lights, "further detailed investigations and modelling will be required" [S-A08a p.14]. It also discounted banning the right turn into Ocean Street and Stuart Street. That is a contrast with the 2026 proposal for signals at Torrens/Seaview and Torrens/Crozier. Inference: the 2026 proposals rest on new, unpublished analysis, or are a change of position.
  • Torrens Street is a DPTI (State) road: Council resolved in 2016 to ask DPTI for a "collaborative and holistic review" of Torrens Street [S-A08a p.5]; signals on Torrens Street would likely need State Department for Infrastructure and Transport approval. Inference.
  • 2017 parking data: about 2,000 on-street spaces in study area (Oct 2015); average weekday occupancy 26-29%, peak 40% over Christmas/New Year; core CBD about 513 on-street spaces, never completely full; McKinlay Street car park 72 spaces rarely at capacity; Esplanade car park 184 spaces "almost empty" on weekdays [S-A08b p.11-12]. The 2017 strategy suggested "Electronic signage ... indicating the number of spaces available" [S-A08b s.5.4].
  • Burke Street rat-running (1500-1650 vpd) [S-A08a p.10]; Grantley Avenue rail crossing partial closure trial endorsed [S-A08a p.7, 14].
  • Council's previous rail-corridor and Goolwa passenger service ideas: the PT map labels a "Modern Victor Harbor-Goolwa passenger service" along Hindmarsh Road [S-A04d].
  • UGMS page says engagement summaries and 29 background docs exist [S-A07a]; population/employment land requirement reports (2023) would hold forecast numbers by growth area.
  • Almond Avenue one-way precedent shows Council can impose one-way by Road Traffic Act s.32 notice with written submissions [S-A07e].
