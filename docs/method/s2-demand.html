Assumptions — S2-demand

Each entry: value, reason, expected sensitivity, and what evidence would replace it. Sourced values are not listed here; they carry their source id in the data files.

All numeric parameters live in ONE file, sim/data/demand/config/default.json (value, unit, basis, note, and sensitivity for assumptions). The tables at the end of this page are generated from it (python -m vhsim.demand.assumptions; a test fails if they drift). Change a value for a sensitivity run without touching code:

python -m vhsim.demand.build --condition summer_peak --year 2025 --growth base --set visitors.summer_vhr_target=13000
python -m vhsim.demand.build --condition all --year all --growth all --config my-variant.json
python -m vhsim.demand.validate --set gravity.beta.supermarket=2.0      # land-use sanity report

Structural assumptions (method, not a single number)

# Assumption Reason Sensitivity Would be replaced by
M1 Study area = the OSM extract bounds: S1's extended extract S-S1-01 (lon 138.55–138.67, lat −35.60 to −35.46; vhsim/demand/params.py falls back to S-E01's −35.52 edge only if S-S1-01 is absent). Every trip end lies inside it. The network (S1) is built from that extract; points outside cannot be routed. Medium: rural trips are cut at the boundary. A larger extract and network.
M2 Zones outside the study area load at a gateway. A mesh block whose representative point is outside the box (and < 50% of its area inside) sends its trips from the gateway on the road nearest to it (OSM ways of the gateway road + rural DIT road geometry S-D05). With S-S1-01: Hindmarsh Tiers and part of Hindmarsh Valley → Hindmarsh Tiers Rd (gw_hti); Mount Jagged → Victor Harbor Rd; Inman Valley → Inman Valley Rd; Back Valley → Range Rd; part of Waitpinga → Waitpinga Rd. About 790 of 17,457 people (2025). Their traffic physically enters on those roads. Low. A wider network.
M3 Growth areas 1–3 and the GARP-only parcels load inside the network (S-S1-01 reaches −35.46; area 2, the northernmost, ends near −35.49). Greenhills Rd and Welch Rd lie wholly inside, so they are no longer gateways (gw_grn, gw_wel exist only for the S-E01 fallback). With S-E01 the northern cells loaded at Victor Harbor Rd / Greenhills Rd / Welch Rd gateways. Network extended by S1 (commit 7cc106e). Medium: cell loading points are polygon representative points, and the growth polygons are approximate (M15). Structure plans with the future street network.
M4 Base 2025 = 2021 mesh-block counts scaled uniformly to ERP 2025 (factor 17,457 / 16,113 = 1.083, dwellings too). No block-level data after 2021. Low–medium: 2021–25 building (~159/yr, S-B10) was partly greenfield (e.g. Hayborough, Encounter Bay estates). 2026 Census mesh-block counts (due ~2027).
M5 Occupied households = persons / 2.1 (S-C01), capped by dwellings. This gives 7,673 in 2021 against 7,156 occupied dwellings counted by the Census, because mesh-block persons include residents of non-private dwellings (aged care). So resident trips are ~7% high. Instruction and no per-block occupancy measure. Medium (all resident volumes). Census SA1 tables of occupied private dwellings; or set population.persons_per_household to 2.25 to match 7,156.
M6 Trip rates are vehicle trips per occupied dwelling, blending the TfNSW regional low-density and seniors-housing rates (S-S2-03) by rates.senior_weight. The surveys exclude trips internal to a subdivision; here every trip leaving a mesh block is a trip. Seniors-housing rates are retirement villages (10am–8pm survey). Only archived Australian rates for a regional low-density town and for seniors. High. Weekday blend 5.37, weekend 3.38 trips/household/day. A Victor Harbor household travel survey; council counts on residential streets (Burke St 1,500–1,650 vpd, S-D21) for checking.
M7 Home-based tours (out and back with a dwell time) plus 15% non-home-based single trips. Purpose shares, outbound hourly priors and dwell times are assumptions; the outbound priors are then fitted so all resident trips follow the S3 hourly profile (sim/data/profiles/hourly_profile.csv: 07–18 observed 2017, other hours assumed by S3). Saturday and summer use the same weekday target shape (no weekend or summer hourly data). No travel survey; the only time-of-day data is the 2017 weekday count. Medium (peak-hour loads). Weekend/summer counts.
M8 Destination choice = gravity: probability ∝ weight / max(d, 0.4 km)^β with d = straight-line × 1.3; β by category. Not calibrated. Simple, transparent, configurable. High for which junctions carry the trips. OD data (e.g. Bluetooth, mobile data) or turning counts via calibration.
M9 POIs: S-E03 mapped to DESIGN categories (+ residential for visits to other households). Private swimming pools (30 of 35 "gym" POIs) dropped. Malls count as shops (Woolworths is its own POI). Schools and hospital supplemented from ABS Education / Hospital mesh blocks. Work = non-residential mesh blocks × jobs/ha + a town-centre weight. Weights are proxies, not measurements. OSM is thin for clinics, cafes and gyms (S-E03 notes). High for supermarket split (validated against weight shares). Floor areas (council rates data), enrolments, employment by destination zone (ABS Census DZN).
M10 Id field conventions beyond DESIGN: dest_category may be home, residential, gateway, holiday_home, caravan_park, accommodation; origin_zone may be a POI id or gateway id for trips not starting at home. S4 resident accessibility should use trips with stratum res, origin_zone a zone (MB… / GA…) and dest_category a POI category (holiday-home visitors also start at MB… zones but are stratum vis). NYE event trips have dest_category beach_foreshore (dest_poi = Warland Reserve): exclude them from beach accessibility for event_nye if an ordinary-day figure is wanted. Keeps every trip's actual origin and destination in its id. None (labelling). —
M11 Gateway volumes: non-resident volume = AADT − residents' expected crossings (rural zones + residents' external trips) − staying visitors' arrivals/departures (term-weekday occupancy), split into thr / ext / vis by gateways.split. Through traffic is paired between gateways with a gravity-type balancing; volume a gateway cannot pair off (Port Elliot Rd has more than Victor Harbor Rd can take) becomes ext. Weekday term = AADT. Only AADT exists (S-D02); no OD or directional data. High for through traffic on Hindmarsh Rd / McCracken roundabout. OD survey or Bluetooth pairs between gateways.
M12 Victor Harbor Rd gateway is on DIT section 28.26–43.21 (AADT 7,700, 12.5% CV; S-D02 via aadt.csv), where the road crosses the north edge of S-S1-01, north of the Hindmarsh Tiers Rd / Virgin Rd junction (RRD 43.21). S-D18's ‘more than 11,000 vehicles/day in the summer peak’ refers to this stretch, so the summer target (≥ 11,000 two-way, visitors.summer_vhr_target) applies at the gateway directly. Hindmarsh Tiers Rd has its own gateway (gw_hti, DIT 04790, 1,200, 12.5% CV). ‘More than’ makes 11,000 a lower bound. DIT AADT and S-D18. Medium (summer volumes in town). A summer count on Victor Harbor Rd / Adelaide Rd.
M13 Summer = residents (school-holiday purpose mix) + all holiday homes mostly occupied + caravan parks/accommodation full + day-trippers scaled so Victor Harbor Rd reaches the target; the same day-tripper factor (x4.2) is applied at the other gateways. Council says population "doubles" (S-C25) but there is no measured peak; the only anchor is S-D18. High. Summer counts; water/waste data (S-C open questions).
M14 NYE: summer_peak plus 1,200 cars to Warland Reserve (5,000 attendees × 0.6 by car / 2.5 per car), arriving 18:00–22:00, 30% leaving 21:15–22:30 and the rest 00:06–01:30 (departures up to 26 h; S4 runs need end ≥ 93,600). No road closures are applied here (they belong in a network scenario). The event and the higher NYE occupancy are added on top of an ordinary summer day: the day-tripper factor is taken from base-year summer_peak, so every gateway carries at least the summer_peak volume. No attendance or travel figures published. High for the event case. Council event management plan / Event Calendar PDF (S-C31 lead).
M15 Growth: dwellings added from 2025 at the S-B10 rates (moderate 185/yr; high 318/410/475 by decade), 76% occupied at 2.1 persons; 40% infill (up to 4,100) spread over residential mesh blocks by dwellings, the rest filling growth areas in council priority order (area 1 up to 2,400 = Deferred Urban Zone; areas 2 and 3 up to 3,600 split by drawn area; then GARP-only parcels at 10/ha). Growth-area polygons are approximate: traced on a 20-px grid from the VHITS map (S-C12 p.2) and georeferenced with three control points (Torrens/Hindmarsh roundabout, McCracken roundabout, Victor Harbor Rd/Waterport Rd junction); OSM Inman Valley Rd, Greenhills Rd, Hindmarsh River, Waterport Rd, Franklin Pde and Tabernacle Rd overlay the map's roads within a few pixels (~50 m). Expect boundary errors of 100–200 m. Each area is cut into ~360 × 440 m cells (zones GA1_00…). Employment growth adds work weight at the two employment areas (clamped into the study area). UGMS GIS layers not obtained. Medium–high for 2041/2051. UGMS 2050 / GARP land-supply GIS layers; structure plans.
M16 Determinism: one numpy generator seeded by --seed; zones and POIs iterate in a fixed order; output sorted by departure. Same seed + config ⇒ identical bytes. Reproducibility. — —

Validation (land use only, before calibration)

python -m vhsim.demand.validate writes sim/data/demand/VALIDATION.md and validation.csv: supermarket trips vs weight, same-suburb and centre/McCracken shopping shares, mean trip length by purpose (assumed plausible ranges), in-area vehicle-km per household and per car, gateway crossings vs DIT AADT (GEH), the hourly shape vs the S3 profile, and trips into the town-centre cordon 07–19 vs the 2017 count (~7,600, S-D19).
