Assumptions and data format — S5 viewer

Each assumption: value, reason, expected sensitivity, what would replace it. The viewer shows simulation outputs; it adds no traffic data of its own. The data contract is sim/VIEWER.md; this file records the exact format and the choices made while implementing it.

Files (exporter output)

python -m vhsim.viewer.export --net <net.xml> --run <run_dir> [--run <seed2> ...] --out <data_dir> --scenario S --condition C --year Y --growth G [--seed N] [--network-name NAME] [--begin s] [--end s] [--bookmark "HH:MM=label"] [--bookmarks file.json] [--pois pois.geojson] [--zones zones.geojson] [--junctions junctions.csv]

<data_dir>/index.json                  {version, networks: {name: "networks/<name>.json"},
                                        runs: [{id, scenario, network, condition, year, growth, seed, label, path}]}
<data_dir>/networks/<name>.json        one per network variant
<data_dir>/runs/<run_id>/meta.json     run_id = "<scenario>__<condition>__<year>-<growth>__s<seed>" (or "__mean")
<data_dir>/runs/<run_id>/lanes.bin.gz
<data_dir>/runs/<run_id>/edge_times.bin.gz

Several --run directories (seeds) are averaged into one run with seed mean (plain mean per lane/edge and interval; a seed with no vehicles on a lane is left out of that lane's speed and density mean). index.json is merged, so runs can be exported one at a time into the same folder.

Safety checks: the exporter refuses to replace networks/<name>.json with a network whose lane/edge ids differ (--force-network overrides; then re-export every run on it), refuses runs whose measured edges are mostly not in --net, and refuses seeds with different output periods. The viewer refuses a run whose network_hash, n_lanes or n_edges do not match the loaded network.

networks/.json (columnar: one array per field, index = position)

  • version, name, hash (16 hex digits of SHA-1 over the edge and lane id lists: the order the run binaries follow), lefthand, bbox [w, s, e, n], projection, routing_vclass (passenger).
  • nodes: id, lon, lat, name (from S1 junctions.csv when given, else null), type (SUMO junction type).
  • edges: id, from/to (node index), length (m), speed_kmh, name, lanes (lane indices), shape (flat [lon, lat, lon, lat, …], 6 decimals ≈ 0.1 m, the edge's centre line from junction to junction), routable (1 if passenger may use it), next (edge indices reachable through a SUMO connection), via_s (free-flow time through the junction's internal lane for each next), bearing (deg, first→last point), slot (drawing offset of the direction line, see below).
  • lanes: id, edge (edge index), index (SUMO lane index, 0 = kerb), bearing, name, speed_kmh, length, slot, shape (the lane's own SUMO shape in lon/lat; the viewer draws lanes from the edge centre line + slot).
  • pois: {id, category, name, lon, lat, node (nearest node a car can enter), snap_m, source}.
  • zones: {id, name, geometry (GeoJSON), edges (routable edges whose midpoint is inside), suburb?, dwellings?, …}.

meta.json

run_id, label, network, network_hash, scenario, condition, year, growth, seed, begin_s, interval_s (900), n_intervals, n_lanes, n_edges, files {lanes, edge_times} (binary layouts, below), bookmarks [{t, label, kind: auto|scenario, detail}], series {delay_veh_h, waiting_veh_h, vehicles_mean, speed_ratio_mean} (one value per interval), summary {loaded, inserted, arrived, teleports, waiting, running, delay_veh_h_total, peak_interval_begin_s}, queue_threshold_vehicles, sources (run dirs), synthetic, notes, created.

Binary layout (both files)

gzip-compressed, little-endian, interval-major planes (value[t * n + i] for lane/edge i in interval t), each plane padded to a 4-byte boundary; decoded = raw * scale unless raw == missing. meta.files.*.planes lists {name, dtype, offset, scale, missing, unit, description}.

file plane dtype scale missing meaning
lanes waiting_s uint16 2 – seconds vehicles were halted on the lane in the interval, summed over vehicles (cap 131070 s ≈ 145 vehicles halted all interval)
lanes vehicles uint16 1 – vehicles entering the lane, incl. lane changes onto it (entered + departed + laneChangedTo); a vehicle can count on two lanes of one road
lanes speed_ratio uint8 1/254 255 time-weighted mean speed / lane limit (SUMO speedRelative), capped at 1; 255 = no vehicles
lanes density uint8 1 255 vehicles per km of lane (cap 254)
edge_times travel_time_s uint16 0.1 65535 SUMO edgeData traveltime; free-flow time where no vehicle; cap 6553.4 s; 65535 = impassable

Size: 6 bytes per lane-interval + 2 per edge-interval before gzip. Victor Harbor scale (~15k lanes, 96 intervals) is about 9 MB raw per run; gzip typically reduces it 5–10×. The sample (1,238 lanes, 76 intervals) is 120–140 kB per run.

V1. Edge travel time = SUMO edgeData traveltime, free-flow where empty

  • Value: mean of the vehicles on the edge in each 15-minute interval (length / time-weighted mean speed, so it includes queueing at the stop line); free-flow time (length / limit) where no vehicle used the edge; never below half the free-flow time (guards one fast sample). Junction crossing time: free-flow time of the internal lane.
  • Reason: contract; edgeData is the only per-interval travel time SUMO gives for every edge.
  • Sensitivity: low on busy roads, higher on rarely used ones (a single slow vehicle sets the value). Seed-mean runs smooth this. Waiting to turn is on the approach edge for all movements, so a long right-turn queue also slows the through movement in the viewer (SUMO lane data would separate them; the routing graph is per edge). The junction time counts only the first internal lane of a connection (slight underestimate for turns with an internal stop).
  • Not represented: road closures (event, NYE). The binary format has an "impassable" code and the router honours it, but the exporter does not write it yet: closed roads would be routed through at free-flow speed. Needed before event scenarios are shown: a closures input (edge, begin, end) to the exporter.

V1a. Sparse samples: less than one edge length travelled (added 22 Nov 2026)

  • Value: SUMO's edgeData traveltime is edge length × vehicle-seconds ÷ the distance the vehicles' fronts travelled in the interval. When the vehicles covered less than one edge length (traveltime > vehicle-seconds), the exporter uses the vehicle-seconds observed plus the free-flow time of the part of the edge not covered (t = sampled + free_flow × (1 − sampled / traveltime)). Intervals in which at least one edge length was travelled are unchanged; a real queue (vehicles on the edge for much of the interval) keeps a long time.
  • Why: SUMO extrapolates from centimetres when the only sample is a vehicle that was already waiting at the stop line as the interval began. Grantley Ave (-42530401#0, 194 m) in the 2025 summer baseline, 12:15–12:30: seed 2 recorded 19,308 s from 11.8 vehicle-seconds and seed 3 9,678 s from 6.5 (one vehicle each, entered 0, left 1); the seed mean hit the format's cap (6,553 s), so the residents' site showed Grantley Ave → Woolworths at 12:30 taking 56.6 minutes today and the council package "53 minutes faster". The vehicles' own exit times (vehroute) show the longest traversal of that street between 12:00 and 13:00 was 70, 97 and 79 s in the three seeds; the rule gives 24 s for the interval (seed mean), and the trip becomes 2.7 minutes today against 3.6 under the package.
  • Extent: in the 2025 summer baseline 517 of 449,568 edge-intervals change by more than 1 s, 17 by more than 5 minutes, all on short local streets or car-park aisles. Across the 99 runs published on 22 Nov 2026 (dry run of the retime tool) every run had some: 1,012 edge-intervals inflated by more than 5 minutes, 3–33 per run. Simulation results (tripinfo, edgeData waiting time, movements, the R14/R15 tables and every S7 number) never used traveltime, so only the viewer and the residents' site were affected. Runs exported before the fix are re-timed in place with python -m vhsim.viewer.retime (edge times only; lane data unchanged).
  • Sensitivity: low; it can under-state a slow single vehicle that was observed for part of a long edge (the rest of the edge is assumed free-flowing), which the next interval's value corrects.

V2. Travel time to a target: arrival-anchored searches, routes driven forward

  • Value: for arrival times t, t + 3¾ min, t + 7½ min, … (4 anchors per 15-minute interval, up to 4 hours) a reverse time-dependent Dijkstra gives every edge's latest entry time and the route (a tree towards the target). The latest entry time is solved exactly on the piecewise-linear edge-time function. For each edge the two anchors whose latest entry times bracket the departure t give two routes; both are driven forward from t with the time-dependent edge times and the faster is shown. Edge times are interpolated linearly between interval centres. Departure = start of the selected interval. Shown value = time from the middle of each road section (route time minus half the section's free-flow time, so a queue at its stop line still counts).
  • Accuracy: every value is the exact time of a real route; it is longer than the true fastest time only when a route neither bracketing anchor chose would be faster. Tested against a forward time-dependent search from each origin on a 12 × 12 grid with sudden 1–10 minute queues on 30% of edges: 5,280 checks, worst error 5.0 s, 1 over max(2%, 5 s). Time: about 12 ms for 9,800 edges in Node; 1–3 ms on the sample network in the browser.
  • Limits: exact only while FIFO holds (an edge's time never falls by more than 15 minutes between two interval centres). Not modelled: drivers re-routing as conditions change, parking search (integration I3), the walk from the car, road closures (V1). Edges with no route are grey.

V3. Snapping the target

  • Value: the target snaps to the nearest node that a car can drive into; a POI likewise. The node's label is its S1 junction name or the street names meeting there.
  • Sensitivity: a target in the middle of a long road snaps to a junction up to half the road's length away.

V3b. Zone shading

  • Value: a zone's value is the median travel time over the routable road sections whose midpoint lies inside it; a zone with none (small mesh blocks) uses up to 3 nearest road sections within 300 m of its centroid (edges_nearest: true); a zone with no road within 300 m (e.g. Granite Island) stays grey.
  • Sensitivity: presentation; a mesh block's median is close to the time from its frontage roads.

V4. Automatic bookmarks (exporter, vhsim/viewer/bookmarks.py)

  • Value: peak interval of network delay (sum of SUMO timeLoss, vehicle-hours per interval); up to two sharpest rises and two falls (each ≥ 15% of the peak and ≥ 0.5 vehicle-hours per 15 min; neighbouring intervals merged; a rise into the peak interval is folded into the peak); the first interval where a junction approach (edge ending at a node with 3+ legs) averages ≥ 5 halted vehicles per lane (waitingTime / 900 / lanes), top 5 approaches by peak queue; the label says "halted vehicles per lane (15-minute average)". In the browser, A-vs-B bookmarks mark the intervals where network delay differs most each way (≥ 10% of the peak and ≥ 0.5 vehicle-hours).
  • Reason: plain, explainable rules. Thresholds are guesses tuned on the sample; --queue-threshold changes the queue one.
  • Sensitivity: presentation only.

V5. Congestion colours

  • Value: speed ratio ≥ 80% of the limit counts as free-flowing (lightest colour); 0% = stopped. Waiting = average halted vehicles (waiting_s / 900). Volume shown per hour. Lanes with no vehicles are grey.
  • Reason: SUMO's urban links rarely average the full limit even when empty (junction slow-downs, speed factor), so a 100% anchor made every road look congested.
  • Sensitivity: presentation only.

V6. Comparing scenarios

  • Value: lanes/edges are matched by SUMO id across networks; roads only in A are drawn dashed ("removed in B"), roads only in B are grey. In difference mode speeds are capped at the free-flow anchor (80% of the limit) and a lane with no vehicles counts as free-flowing, so an empty lane in A and a lightly used one in B do not show as a slow-down; queue, volume and density count no vehicles as zero. A road with a route in A but none in B shows as the largest increase (darkest red). When B's time window does not cover the selected time, B shows no data (it is not clamped to its nearest interval); runs with different interval lengths cannot be compared. Differences inside a dead band (15 s, 5 percentage points, half a vehicle, 3 vehicles/km, 20 vehicles/h) are shown as no change, because single-seed runs differ by that much by chance.
  • Sensitivity: with one seed per scenario, small differences are noise; use seed-mean runs (several --run). If a scenario patch renames edges (e.g. splits one), those edges will not match and show as removed + new.

V7. Lane drawing offsets

  • Value: lanes are drawn from the edge's junction-to-junction centre line (getRawShape) with a pixel offset (slot lane widths, MapLibre line-offset): two-way roads put each direction on its driving side (left in left-hand networks, read from the net's lefthand attribute), kerb lane outermost, 0.15 lane widths gap at the centre line; one-way roads are centred. Widths are exaggerated below zoom 16 and true to scale (3.2 m) above.
  • Sensitivity: presentation only. One-way roads imported with spreadType=right are drawn half a road width off.

V8. Sample data (sim/data/viewer/sample/, python -m vhsim.viewer.sample)

  • Value: NOT RESULTS. A quick netconvert of the S-E01 OSM extract clipped to 138.598–138.640 E, 35.540–35.568 S (--lefthand, OSM speeds, no S1 fixes); 15,000 synthetic car/truck trips (shape of the S3 hourly profile, made-up purposes: home ↔ centre/shops, 15% through traffic, 250 school trips per school each way at drop-off and pick-up); 2041-high = 1.3 × trips; summer peak = beach trips instead of school trips; "test_variant" = Victoria Street one-way (south/west-bound edges deleted) and Esplanade 40 km/h. One seed per run. Places from S-E03; zones are a 3 × 3 grid of test cells. Every run is marked synthetic and the UI shows a banner.
  • Reason: real network (S1), demand (S2) and calibrated runs are not ready; the viewer needed congestion and a before/after difference to be built and tested against.

V9. External libraries

  • MapLibre GL JS 5.6.0 from cdnjs (viewer/index.html); no build step. The tests load the same version from viewer/node_modules so they run offline. Background map: OpenStreetMap raster tiles, off by default.

Residents' site (viewer/residents/)

R1. Trip times

  • Value: fastest time-dependent route (forward search, routeFromNode) from the simulated junction nearest each end of the trip, departing at the trip's time, on the run for that option, day type, year and growth. Places more than 800 m from any junction count as "outside the simulated area". Door-to-door parts (parking, walking) are not included.
  • Sensitivity: small for trips between named places; a home picked in a paddock snaps to the nearest road.

R2. "About the same" band (grey)

  • Value: a trip is "about the same" when the option's time is within the larger of 30 seconds or 5% of today's time; outside it, faster (green) or slower (red). Route sections on the map use the larger of 3 seconds or 10% of the section's time. Colours: Okabe-Ito bluish green and vermillion, always with words and ▲/▼/= symbols.
  • Single-seed runs (some future years are 1 seed): the band doubles to the larger of 60 seconds or 10%, and the page says the result is from a single run and less certain. The heat-map difference uses the same band.
  • Reason: results are seed means, and the 15-minute averaging of section times moves trip times by tens of seconds even with no change. Not yet measured from the seed spread: when per-seed runs are exported, the band can be set to about twice the seed-to-seed standard deviation of trip times.
  • Sensitivity: presentation; it decides only which small changes are called "about the same".

R3. Day types

  • Weekday (school term) → weekday_term, Saturday → saturday, Summer holidays → summer_peak. Day types or years without a run are shown as "not simulated yet"; nothing is interpolated or borrowed from another day or year. 2025 is "today" (growth base) for both growth settings.

R4. Privacy

  • Trips and saved places live only in localStorage (key vh-residents-v1), every access wrapped in try/catch; if storage is blocked the site works for the visit and says nothing is saved. No trip data goes in URLs, requests or logs; all routing runs in the browser on the published results. Place search uses the published places list (no external address lookup). The only third-party requests are the map library (cdnjs) and, if a future version turns it on, background map tiles.

R5. Data the site loads

  • networks/<name>.lite.json (routing-only, written by the exporter, or backfilled with python -m vhsim.viewer.lite --data <dir>; falls back to the full network file) and each run's edge_times.bin.gz. The "all options at a glance" table loads every option's network and only runs when asked.

Base map (viewer/basemap/, vhsim/viewer/basemap.py, vhsim/viewer/terrain.py)

Provenance, licences and hand-placed labels: viewer/basemap/SOURCES.md. These choices only change how the map looks; no simulation result depends on them.

V10. Building heights (3-D buildings)

  • Value: OSM height when present (none in G-01), else building:levels × 3 m + 1 m (4 buildings), else a default by type: house / detached / semi-detached / terrace / residential, and building=yes up to 800 m²: 4.5 m; garage / carport / shed / roof / hut: 2.8 m; apartments: 10 m; retail / commercial / supermarket / office (or any building with a shop tag): 6.5 m; school / college: 7 m; church or place of worship: 10 m; hospital: 9 m; industrial / warehouse: 7 m; hotel: 10 m; building=yes over 800 m²: 6.5 m. Rounded to 0.5 m.
  • Reason: 97% of G-01 buildings are building=yes with no height or levels. Victor Harbor is mostly single-storey detached houses (about 3 m walls plus a pitched roof, so about 4.5 m to the ridge); footprints over 800 m² are shops, halls and sheds with high single-storey roofs. The type defaults are typical heights for one-storey outbuildings, two-storey schools and shops, and three-storey apartment and hotel blocks.
  • Sensitivity: presentation only. A two-storey house is drawn about 2–3 m too low.
  • Class (c): from the building value, then amenity / shop / tourism tags; plain building=yes with no other tag is other (not guessed to be a house), so most buildings are other.
  • Buildings under 12 m² are dropped (mostly garden sheds and water tanks); outlines are simplified with a 0.5 m tolerance and snapped to the 1e-5° (about 1 m) output grid.

V11. Base-map generalisation

  • Value: areas and lines are clipped to the G-01 box (138.57–138.66 E, 35.53–35.598 S), where the extract is complete; outside it the map shows the terrain only. Buildings and pier outlines crossing the box edge are kept whole. Simplification tolerances (in metres, before snapping to 1e-5°): sea and inland water 1 m, land use 1.5 m, lines 1 m, piers 0.5 m. Land-use areas under 20 m² are dropped. landuse=construction (10 new housing estates) and landuse=quarry (1) are left out: they have no class in the map style. leisure=nature_reserve, swimming_pool and playground areas are left out too.
  • Label zooms: islands and the bay 12 (islands under 10 ha 13); town suburbs (1,000+ G-NAF addresses) 12, rural localities 11; landmarks, rivers, peaks and Petrel Cove 13; Chiton Rocks and the hospital 14; golf courses and parks over 20 ha 13.5; other parks, the large named oval and schools 14.5.
  • Sea outside the G-01 box: from the terrain tiles: below 1 m and connected to the OSM sea or to the south/west edge of the area; offshore land under 5 m or under 2 ha counts as sea; simplified by 40 m (details in viewer/basemap/SOURCES.md). The 1 m and 5 m thresholds deal with noise in the source near the coast; a lower coast (beaches under 1 m) would be drawn as sea, a 30–50 m effect at this scale.
  • Terrain: sea-floor elevations are set to 0 (a flat sea, no bathymetry shading) and elevations are rounded to 1/8 m. Zoom 12 (about 15 m per pixel) covers the town only; zooms 10–11 cover the whole network.
  • Sensitivity: presentation only.
