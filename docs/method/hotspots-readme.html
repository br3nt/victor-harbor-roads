Hotspot evidence pack

Hotspot by hotspot: today's roads, the council's package and the best tested alternative, with numbers, side-by-side animations of the same 15 simulated minutes, and congestion maps. Built for an onboarding page and a slide deck. Method and assumptions: sim/assumptions/S8-hotspots.md.

Years. This year is 2026. The model's "today" is built on 2025 data and is labelled now (2025 data). 2031 is in about 5 years (moderate or high growth, 1 run each); 2041 high growth is in about 15 years (term weekday only: the 2041 summer day jammed for every option tried there, i.e. today's roads, CP, BP and the four-lane ring road; the N options were not tried). Now = mean of 3 runs. The numbers compare options on the same traffic; they are not forecasts (Victoria St is over-loaded about 1.5× in the model, the town centre somewhat under-loaded).

Open it. cd viewer && node test/serve.mjs then http://127.0.0.1:4418/hotspots/. One hotspot per entry in the list; #h=<id>&s=<set> links straight to one.

Terms. Wait = seconds per vehicle on the approach beyond its free-flow time (the R15 approach delay). Time stopped = vehicle-hours a day standing still on the hotspot's lanes. Queue = vehicles standing still, averaged over the busiest 15 minutes (7.5 m a vehicle). Summer = the summer peak day.

With the council package: about 5,200 (82 trucks, 97 caravans); the loop alone 7,400 (162 trucks, 118 caravans). N9 and BP leave it as today. - Term weekday, now: 1,030 → 2,740 a day with CP; trucks 10 → 66. - 2031 (in about 5 years), high growth, summer: CP about 5,800 a day (63 trucks, 90 caravans) against 3,100 (14 trucks) today. The loop alone backs up badly here: 388 vehicle-hours stopped a day at this spot (today's roads: 7), with worst approach waits over 100 s at Eyre Tce / Bridge Tce and Coral St / Ocean St. - Time stopped at the spot, summer now: 3.6 vehicle-hours today, 11.0 with CP.

These are single runs and swing a lot here (today's roads come out worse at moderate growth than at high, and N9 is 41% better at one growth and 3% worse at the other), so only the consistent result stands: N7 is about 40% lower than today's roads at both growths. That is traffic taking the improved ring road instead of Victoria St, not a fix for the corridor itself. - Caveat: Victoria St carries about 1.5× its counted traffic in the model and the car parks are simplified; this is the least reliable hotspot.

Caveats

  • One seed per animation and per 2031 result. Now (3 seeds) summer runs vary ±5–12% between seeds; single 2031 runs at busy hotspots swing much more (Victoria St, Maude St, the oval roundabout), so treat a 2031 difference as real only when it shows at both growths.
  • Give-way gap acceptance is SUMO's model with the A-INT-02 impatience settings; not checked on site.
  • Route choice by travel time only, so diversions into residential streets may be on the high side.
  • New junctions (N3, N4, N7) are geometric approximations, not designs; N4 sits beside a level crossing.
  • Queues in the numbers are 15-minute averages; the animations show moment-to-moment queues, counted only inside the picture, and draw trucks and caravans 1.5 times their size so they can be picked out.
  • Approach waits include about 1 s of crossing the previous junction, and leave out time waiting inside the junction (right-turners at the turn line), so right-turn waits are somewhat understated.
  • The hotspot areas here are wider than the S7 diagnosis's in places (oval roundabout, McCracken, Torrens St), so their vehicle-hour totals are higher than S7's; compare options within these tables.

Jammed runs

None of the 16 new 2031 runs (N3, N4, N7, N9 × moderate/high × weekday/summer) jammed (0–29 teleports each, under the 5% limit). Flagged as jammed in the hotspot files: the 2041 high-growth summer day for today's network, CP and BP (stopped before the end of the day, A-INT-05). The one-way loop alone (C3) at 2031 high growth, summer, did not jam by the batch rule (1,043 teleports, 1.1%) but shows extreme queues at hotspots 1, 2 and 5.
