S7 — Where the delay is, and targeted options aimed at it

Stream S7 (integration, 21–22 Nov 2026). Part 1 diagnoses where the simulated delay is on today's network; part 2 defines options aimed at those places (ids N2–N9 in sim/SCENARIOS.md); part 3 gives their results against today's network, the council's package (CP) and Brent's package (BP). Each assumption has a value, a reason and how sensitive results are to it.

Tools: python -m vhsim.integration.bottlenecks diagnose|hotspots ... (from sim/; outputs in sim/data/results/s7_diagnosis/). Measure: vehicle-hours stopped (SUMO edgeData waitingTime, speed below 0.1 m/s), the same quantity as the "time stopped" in report_2025_base/summary.csv but summed per edge, seed mean of 3 runs. The edgeData total is a little below the trip total (440 vs 460 vehicle-hours in summer 2025) because edgeData is written without internal lanes (vhsim.metrics.run): time stopped inside a junction, mostly right-turners waiting at the turn line, is on no edge. Per-approach and hotspot figures therefore slightly undercount right-turn waits; the trip totals in part 3 include them.

What the delay is

  1. Side-street right turns onto busy one-lane arterials are most of it. Movement detail (movements.csv, summer 2025): Bridge Tce traffic turns left into the 17 m Grantley Ave crossing link (50 s mean wait) and then right onto Hindmarsh Rd (20 s) — the link holds two cars, so the right-turners' wait backs up into Bridge Tce. Wattle Dr right onto Hindmarsh Rd: 48 s. Granite St right onto Victoria St: 45 s. Coral St right onto Torrens St: 27 s. Crozier Rd onto Armstrong Rd: left 32 s and right 25 s (left-turners queue behind right-turners in the one shared lane).
  2. Victoria St between Bay Rd and Torrens St is a chain of closely spaced give-way junctions and car-park accesses on a one-lane-each-way street carrying 10,000–12,000 vehicles a day in summer: 2,400 a day turn left into Oval Rd (17 s each, slowing the through lane), 980 turn left into the IGA car park (1,400 at 2041 high growth), the car-park exit waits 41 s, Granite St's right turn 45 s, and the westbound queue from the Bay Rd roundabout entry reaches back 120 m. Caution: the calibration over-loads Victoria St (1.53 x the DIT count, A-INT-04) and models the car parks with a few OSM service roads, so this hotspot is the least trustworthy; a real fix here needs the car-park access design, which the model cannot test.
  3. The roundabouts are not the main problem today. Mean approach delay at the two Torrens St roundabouts (Seaview/Eyre and Crozier) is 3–8 s on most approaches; the exceptions are Eyre Tce (13 s weekday, 20 s summer), Seaview Rd (8 / 13 s) and Crozier Rd from the west (5 / 8 s); at Torrens/Victoria/Wills, Wills St 7 / 10 s; at McCracken the three main legs 3–8 s (a minor leg 9 / 14 s); at Bay Rd 2–7 s (report_2025_base/intersections.csv). This is why the council's signals (C4) and Brent's slip lanes (B5) do not help: they replace or bypass junctions that are not where the queues are.
  4. At 2041 high growth the ring road's give-way junctions fail. Armstrong Rd / Crozier Rd becomes the worst junction in town (54 vehicle-hours; Crozier Rd waits 53 s a vehicle, its left-turners 92 s); Armstrong Rd / Greenhills Rd grows from 0.5 (2025 weekday) to 12. The growth areas load onto the rural roads north of the ring (Waggon Rd, Day Rd, Greenhills Rd, Fuller Rd; S2 M3/M15) and cross or join the ring road at these give-way junctions. Both packages make this worse: CP 92, BP 106, B3 alone 92 vehicle-hours at Armstrong/Crozier (more traffic on the ring road, fewer gaps for Crozier Rd).

2. Options and their assumptions

General: every option is a netconvert patch over the baseline (vhsim.scenarios.defs, patches in sim/data/networks/patches/<id>/, built with python -m vhsim.scenarios.build <id> --smoke), then the A-INT-01 turn bays as for every scenario. All pass the connectivity checks and the 30-minute smoke run.

S7-01. New single-lane roundabouts (N2, N4, N5)

  • Value: the priority junction is replaced by a ring of one node per leg on a circle of radius r round the old junction, one-lane ring edges running clockwise, approach and exit edges trimmed to the circle (roundabout() in defs.py, geometry in vhsim/scenarios/s7.py). The old junction's connections are dropped and netconvert computes the roundabout's (every movement stays possible; U-turns become possible). Ring speed follows the baseline's own single-lane roundabouts, v² = 2.9 r (m/s; 20 m → 27 km/h, 12 m → 21 km/h, 15 m → 24 km/h). Ring nodes are recorded as aliases of the named junction (meta/summary.json junction_aliases) so the R15 tables find them.
  • Radii: N2 Armstrong/Crozier 20 m (as McCracken 18.8 m and Armstrong/George Main 25.4 m, rural 100 km/h road); N5 Armstrong/Greenhills 15 m (the next Armstrong Rd node is 30 m north; 20 m would leave a 9 m stub before it, 15 m about 14 m); N4 Hindmarsh/Grantley 12 m (urban, between the Torrens St roundabouts' 9–11 m and McCracken).
  • N4 placement: the crossing link from Hindmarsh Rd to Bridge Tce is 17 m long. The ring centre is moved 8 m west (away from the railway) so about 13 m of link remains between the ring and Bridge Tce. Real design would need the roundabout's footprint west of Hindmarsh Rd (land) and level-crossing protection: queues from the ring would otherwise stand on the crossing (they already do today with the give-way). Inference — no design exists.
  • Not modelled: approach speed reduction signs before a rural roundabout (SUMO slows vehicles at the ring anyway), entry deflection, pedestrians.
  • Sensitivity: moderate for delay at the junction (gap acceptance at a roundabout entry vs a give-way T is SUMO's model, with the A-INT-02 impatience settings); low elsewhere.

S7-02. Approach flares and turn lanes (N3, N8)

  • Value: the approach edge is split so its last 40 m (at most half the edge; netconvert then cuts the junction area, leaving 28–41 m) have two lanes, the new end edge named <id>.bay like the A-INT-01 bays. N3 (Crozier Rd at Armstrong Rd): kerb lane left turns, median lane right turns. N8 (Victoria St eastbound into Oval Rd 173449383#0 and into the IGA car park 173449383#3): kerb lane for the left turn only, median lane for through traffic, so slowing left-turners no longer hold up the through lane.
  • Inference: lengths are our choice (a typical short turn lane); widths and land not checked.
  • Sensitivity: low-moderate; SUMO's lane-change model decides how early drivers move over.

S7-03. No right turn out (N6)

  • Value: the right-turn movement out of Granite St (onto Victoria St), Coral St (onto Torrens St) and Wattle Dr (onto Hindmarsh Rd) is removed.
  • Selection: the side-street right turn with the most vehicle-hours on each of the three arterials through or next to the town centre (summer 2025 movements: Granite St 13.0, Wattle Dr 10.6, Coral St 8.0 vehicle-hours a day). Not included: Burke St onto Crozier Rd (14.7; Crozier Rd is a local road, not an arterial) and Bartel Blvd / Harbour View Tce onto Bay Rd (10.3 / 8.9; Bay Rd was left for a later variant). Renown Ave onto Hindmarsh Rd has a longer wait per vehicle (38 s) than Coral St but fewer vehicles. Drivers re-route (A-INT-03 rerouting) — typically left then round the next roundabout, or via a parallel side street.
  • Sensitivity: where the displaced right-turners go decides the result; the model chooses by travel time, real drivers may also pick routes through residential streets the model under-uses.

S7-04. Cost classes

  • Value: $ under $0.5 M (line marking, signs, short widening), $$ $0.5–3 M (one roundabout on an existing road reserve), $$$ $3–10 M (two roundabouts, or one needing land and rail-crossing work).
  • Why: an order-of-magnitude label so options can be compared; not from a source and not a cost estimate. Replace with council or DIT estimates.
  • Sensitivity: none for the simulation results; affects only how options are ranked for value.

S7-05. Options considered and not built

  • Roundabout metering signals (holiday peaks only): metering helps an entry starved by one dominant circulating flow. Only the Eyre Tce entry at the Torrens/Seaview/Eyre roundabout shows that pattern (13–20 s a vehicle, about 10 vehicle-hours on a summer day); metering the Hindmarsh Rd flow would move part of that delay to Hindmarsh Rd. The possible gain is small next to the hotspots above.
  • Properly designed signals (turn bays, protected turns) at the C4 sites: the two roundabouts carry their load with 3–8 s average delay on most approaches (Eyre Tce and Seaview Rd 13–20 s in summer); their hotspot totals are 5–30 vehicle-hours a day, so even a signal design that removed all of it would be a small gain, and C4 as built added 20–25%. Not a target.
  • Link road giving growth area 1 a second way out: the demand model loads the growth areas onto existing rural roads (no estate streets, S2 M3/M15), so any new link's connection points would be invented. N2/N5 treat the junctions that growth traffic actually uses in the model.
  • Signed caravan/heavy-vehicle route; seasonal traffic management: heavy vehicles on the foreshore are few in every run (R23a table, foreshore.csv) and seasonal management (event road closures, marshals) has no measurable model counterpart.
  • C5 outer freight ring road: see the end of part 3.

Caveats

  • No option touches the largest hotspot, the Victoria St corridor (shopping-centre accesses on a one-lane street), and that hotspot is the model's least reliable (Victoria St over-loaded 1.5 x, car parks as OSM service roads). The Hindmarsh Rd side-street right turns (about 50 vehicle-hours on a summer day) are untouched too. Even N9 leaves about 80% of the summer delay where it is.
  • Gap acceptance at give-way junctions and roundabout entries is SUMO's model with the A-INT-02 impatience settings; the roundabout-vs-give-way comparisons (N2, N4, N5, N7) depend on it. It has not been checked against observed waits.
  • The roundabouts, flares and turn lanes are geometric approximations, not designs; N4 sits beside a level crossing and would need crossing protection and land west of Hindmarsh Rd.
  • Summer runs vary ±5–12% between seeds; 2041 option runs have 1–3 seeds.
  • Growth demand has no estate streets; the 2041 summer peak was not run (it does not finish for any option, A-INT-05) and the optional 2031 high summer run was not done.

Grantley Ave in the viewer: a 53-minute difference that was an export artefact (22 Nov 2026)

The residents' site showed Grantley Ave → Woolworths on a 2025 summer peak day at 12:30 as 53 minutes faster under the council package than today (56.6 minutes today). Checked against the three 2025 summer runs of today's network: - Not a real queue. The vehicles' own exit times (vehroute) give the longest traversal of the Grantley Ave section before Hindmarsh Rd (-42530401#0, 194 m) between 12:00 and 13:00 as 70, 97 and 79 s in seeds 1–3 (22, 15 and 15 vehicles). The queue there is a normal side-street wait of under a minute and a half. - Not a stuck vehicle or a teleport. No teleport on Grantley Ave, Bridge Tce or the crossing link in any seed (5, 2 and 1 teleports in the whole day elsewhere); the A-INT-02 impatience and junction-blocker settings are not involved. - The cause: SUMO's edgeData traveltime (length × vehicle-seconds ÷ distance the vehicles' fronts travelled). In 12:15–12:30, seeds 2 and 3 each saw a single vehicle that had been waiting at the stop line since before 12:15; it moved a few centimetres and left after 12 s and 7 s. SUMO extrapolated 19,308 s and 9,678 s; the seed mean reached the export format's cap (6,553 s), and the viewer interpolated that into the 12:30 departure. - Fix (export layer, assumption V1a in S5-viewer.md): when the vehicles covered less than one edge length in the interval, the edge time is the vehicle-seconds observed plus free flow over the rest of the edge. Grantley Ave at 12:15 becomes 24 s (seed mean); Grantley Ave → Woolworths at 12:30 becomes 2.7 minutes today against 3.6 minutes under the council package (the package is about a minute slower, not 53 minutes faster). In the 2025 summer baseline the rule changes 517 of 449,568 edge-intervals by more than a second and 17 by more than 5 minutes, all on short local streets and car-park aisles. Published runs are re-timed with python -m vhsim.viewer.retime. - N4/N9 do not depend on it. Every number in this note (time stopped from tripinfo, hotspot vehicle-hours from edgeData waitingTime, approach delays from vehroute exit times) is computed without traveltime, so the N4/N9 summer results stand (time stopped −7% and −18%; Bridge Tce at the crossing 51 → 16 s a vehicle). The artefact only affected the viewer: it put the same false 56-minute value on that section in today's network and in N4 (both 56.4–56.6 minutes at 12:30), and none in N9 or the council package, so before the fix the residents' site overstated the gain of N9 and CP at that one section and time, and understated N4's.

C5 (outer freight ring road) — not built here; see S9

Built afterwards from the described end points and existing roads: sim/assumptions/S9-ring-road.md. The original reason for leaving it out follows. The indicative alignment (S-A04b) could not be placed reliably. Georeferencing the map from road junctions the map labels (Greenhills/Hutchinson Rd, Hutchinson/Hindmarsh Tiers Rd, Greenhills/Day Rd) gave scales that disagree by nearly a factor of two (the labelled junctions cannot be pinned on the map), so the new sections (south-west across the Inman River to Greenhills Rd, and the north/north-east section to Victor Harbor Rd) would have invented end points. The parts that follow existing roads (Greenhills Rd from Day Rd north, Hutchinson Rd, then Hindmarsh Tiers Rd) are already in the network at 50–100 km/h. In this demand model C5 could only attract through traffic between Inman Valley Rd and Victor Harbor Rd, a small flow, and would not touch the bottlenecks in part 1. It needs the council's alignment (or a careful georeferencing like S2's for the growth map) before it is worth simulating.
