Stream C: Population, dwellings, growth areas, holiday and seasonal demand

Retrieved 2026-11-21. Sources are listed in research/C-population-sources.tsv; archived copies are in sources/C/. Items marked "(not archived)" were seen only in a web search summary and must be checked against the original before use. Victor Harbor LGA code (ASGS 2021) is 48050.

1. Summary

  • Resident population now. ABS 2021 Census usual residents: 16,139 [S-C01]. ABS Estimated Resident Population (ERP) at 30 June 2025: 17,457, up 1.94% on 2024 (17,124) [S-C03]. Series 2018 to 2025: 15,248; 15,465; 15,724; 15,996; 16,720; 16,869; 17,124; 17,457 [S-C03].
  • Dwellings. 9,746 private dwellings, of which 7,156 occupied (76.2%) and 2,245 unoccupied (23.9%) at the 2021 Census [S-C01]. Mesh-block sum for the LGA is 9,724 dwellings and 16,113 persons [S-C30]. Victor Harbor township: 24.6% unoccupied [S-C14 s.4.3.4]. Unoccupied share was 29.1% in 2016 [S-C15].
  • Household size and cars. 2.1 people per household (SA 2.4); 1.7 motor vehicles per dwelling; 5.4% of occupied dwellings have no vehicle, 44.5% one, 34.2% two, 14.7% three or more [S-C01].
  • Journey to work (Census day). Car as driver 62.8% (3,379); car as passenger 5.1%; public transport 0.7% (35); walked only 3.1%; worked at home 11.3%; did not go to work 13.2% [S-C01].
  • Age. Median age 60; 65 and over is about 42% (6,813 people, sum of 65-69 to 85+ bands) [S-C01]. Council says 43% on the wider South Coast (Victor Harbor to Goolwa) [S-C10 p.07].
  • Forecasts differ widely (see section 2.1): about 18.8-19.1k by 2031 (council consultant model), 23.9k by 2041 for the Victor Harbor SA2 (state projection, not archived), 25,292 by 2041 (council quoting the Greater Adelaide Regional Plan, GARP), and 25,000 to 36,000 by 2051 (council), with the VHITS fact sheet saying "approximately double" to about 36,000 by 2051 [S-C12 p.1].
  • Northern growth. The Integrated Transport Strategy fact sheet shows three numbered residential growth areas north of the existing town, around the Hindmarsh River, Greenhills Road and Waggon Road, in the council's order of priority (GARP and UGMS), plus GARP-only residential areas on Inman Valley Road and south-west of the town, and employment land by Waterport Road [S-C12 p.2]. Dwelling counts and staging per area were not found (the adopted UGMS PDF could not be downloaded; see Gaps).
  • Summer peak. Council's 2025/26 Annual Business Plan: "in the summer months the population doubles" [S-C25]. Council's Long Term Financial Plan reportedly says 30,000+ in peak summer months (not archived, S-C35). A .id article says some estimates put it at three times (trebles), an older figure from about 2006-2010 [S-C06]. Around 40% of ratepayers do not live in the council area [S-C25].
  • Short-term rental. About 210 Airbnb and 379 Stayz listings in April 2023 [S-C14 s.4.5; S-C15].
  • Term dates and events are in section 2.6 and 2.7. Schoolies is 20-22 November 2026 at Warland Reserve [S-C32].

Northern growth area (coordinator's lead). - The VHITS fact sheet (Oct 2026) says new residential and employment growth areas "have been identified in the State Government's Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) and Council's Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS)" and will "gradually be developed as the population grows" [S-C12 p.2]. - Its map (saved as S-C38) shows, as read from the image: three residential growth areas numbered by council priority (GARP and UGMS), forming a continuous band north of the existing urban area. Area 1 lies closest to town on both sides of the Hindmarsh River and Waggon Road; Area 2 is the largest and lies north-east along the Hindmarsh River; Area 3 is north-west towards Greenhills Road. Also shown: "Residential Growth (GARP only)" in two parcels, one along Inman Valley Road and Inman River (north-west) and one south-west of the town near Tabernacle Road; "Employment Growth Area (GARP and UGMS)" north-east near Waterport Road, and "Employment Growth Area (GARP only)" further east [S-C12 p.2]. Inference: map positions are read by eye; the exact parcel boundaries need the GARP/UGMS GIS layers. - The ring road map (p.5) shows the northern boundary as Hutchinson Road, the western edge as Greenhills Road, with Waggon Road, Day Road and Waterport Road inside or at the edge of the area, and the Hindmarsh River running through it. Option 1 is a new outer ring road around this edge (existing Ring Road becomes a 60 km/h link road). Option 2 keeps the current Ring Road and adds a "new activity centre" north of it, because the existing Ring Road "creates a barrier between the existing south and future growing north communities" [S-C12 p.5]. - Planning instruments: GARP (in effect 17 March 2025; identifies Victor Harbor as a future "Satellite City") [S-C25]; council UGMS 2050 adopted 22 September 2025 [S-C34]. The land is not yet zoned for urban use: the 2023 land report says rezoning is required before development (see below) [S-C14 s.5.1]. 2.5 E16: Holiday homes, short-term rental, peak population

  • Unoccupied dwellings: 2,245 (23.9%) in 2021, against 10.8% for SA and about 7% in Greater Adelaide [S-C01; S-C14 s.4.3.4]. Township 24.6% [S-C14]. 29.1% in 2016, so the share has fallen [S-C15]. The Future Ready paper puts it at 22% and calls them "unoccupied holiday homes" [S-C10 housing page].
  • Absentee owners: "around 40% of our ratepayers do not live in the Council area" [S-C25 Our Community]. An earlier UGMS review found "up to 25% of land owners are holding on to vacant land" and that many properties are holiday "shacks"; "Over the summer holiday season most of the dwellings are occupied" [S-C14 s.4.3.4].
  • Short-term rental: "approximately 210 listings on AirBnB and approximately 379 listings on Stayz (April 2023)", described as "up to 5.1% of unoccupied dwellings" [S-C14 s.4.5]. Inference: 589 listings is about 26% of 2,245 unoccupied dwellings (6% of all dwellings), so the 5.1% as stated does not reconcile; listings may overlap between platforms. Inside Airbnb: no Victor Harbor dataset checked (the site was not searched; low prior).
  • Peak population: "in the summer months the population doubles. Attracting around 1.2 million visitors annually" [S-C25]. Long Term Financial Plan reportedly "30,000+" in peak summer months (not archived, S-C35); 30,000 against 16,139 is about 1.9 times. .id: "population trebles" in summer, from older data [S-C06]. Wikipedia and other secondary pages repeat "almost triples" (not used as evidence).
  • Inference for the model: 2,245 empty dwellings at 2.1 people each would add about 4,700 people, about 29% of residents. A doubling (about +16,000) therefore needs far more than holiday homes being used by their owners: caravan and holiday parks, short-term lets, visiting friends and relatives, and day visitors. This is why a simulation should treat the summer peak as a separate demand layer, not scale household travel.

2.6 E17a: Visitors

  • Tourism contributed an estimated $663 million to the Fleurieu Peninsula regional economy in 2021 and directly employed about 3,700 people; $679 million in September 2022 [S-C15 s.5.9, citing SATC].
  • SA Tourism Commission: Fleurieu Peninsula had 893,000 domestic overnight visitors in the year to December 2021 and 2.6 million day trips with spend of $230 million [S-C15 s.5.9].
  • Council says about 1.2 million visitors annually [S-C25]. This is a council statement without a stated period or definition.
  • Not found: quarterly visitor numbers. SATC "Visit to regions" reports (year to December 2025 and March 2026) and a Fleurieu value-of-tourism fact sheet exist at tourism.sa.gov.au (listed in search results, not opened). SATC notes that the December 2025 results use the new Domestic Tourism Statistics and are not comparable with earlier National Visitor Survey results.
