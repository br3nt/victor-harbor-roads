# Stream B: Decision provenance and due diligence (Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy)

Stream B, compiled 21 November 2026 (Adelaide time). Sources are listed in `research/B-provenance-sources.tsv` and archived in `sources/B/`.

The consultation is formally titled the **Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS)** [S-B01; S-B03 p.75]. The town-centre package (30 km/h, shared zones, a one-way outer loop, traffic lights, digital parking signage) is one "Vision Element" of the four in the strategy. The Ring Road options form another [S-B02 pp.3-5].

Access note: www.victor.sa.gov.au and lga.sa.gov.au return a Cloudflare challenge to curl. Everything was read and downloaded through an isolated headless browser context with no form submission and no sign-in.

---

## 1. Summary

- **What started it.** The proximate trigger was a Motion on Notice from **Cr Angela Schiller**, seconded by **Cr Marilyn Henderson**. It was carried unanimously on 15 December 2025 (OC3792025) and asked Administration to "commence a Traffic and Transport Investigation and Analysis Report" in 2025/26 as phase one, with phase two to be costed as a 2026/27 budget bid [S-B07 p.15].
  - The carried text is stronger than the notice version, which only sought costings. The officer report had also proposed a narrower alternative tied to UGMS actions 3.1A/3.3A/1.1B [S-B06 pp.318-321]. The minutes do not record how the wording changed [S-B07 p.15].
- **The policy parent.** The parent document is the Urban Growth Management Strategy 2050 (UGMS), adopted 22 September 2025 (OC2592025, moved Cr Kemp, seconded Cr Burns) [S-B09 p.8; S-B03 p.75]. That sits under the State's Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) high-growth targets [S-B04 SMEC pp.7-9].
- **Procurement.** Council advertised tender CVH058153 from 24 January to 23 February 2026 [S-B21, secondary]. It engaged **SMEC** (contract C0000243), with **URPS** supporting stakeholder engagement [S-B03 p.77, p.79; S-B04 SMEC p.3].
  - No public council report on the tender evaluation or award was found. The confidential items register lists no VHITS or SMEC item [S-B39].
- **Phasing and budget.** The project "was initiated in April 2026" in two phases [S-B03 p.77]. Phase 1 (an audit of the existing network plus a sketch model) was submitted as Revision 1 on 30 June 2026 and Revision 2 on 21 August 2026 [S-B04 SMEC p.4]. Phase 2 is budgeted at $100,000 under C0000243 [S-B03 p.79]. The 2026/27 Annual Business Plan shows $120,000 [S-B17 p.41; S-B16 p.56]. The Phase 1 cost is not disclosed.
- **Elected members' involvement.** Members were briefed three times, all in open session: 11 May 2026 (60 min), 15 July 2026 (60 min) and 12 October 2026 (30 min) [S-B11; S-B12; S-B13]. No notes or minutes of these briefings are published.
- **How fast the options appeared.** The draft consultation maps first appear in the 26 October 2026 agenda, 14 days after the 12 October briefing [S-B03 pp.88-96]. Council approved consultation that night without a recorded division (OC3572026, moved Cr Kemp, seconded Cr Mann) [S-B05 p.8].
- **Where the one-way loop and 30 km/h came from.** Neither appears in SMEC's Phase 1 report (August 2026). That report contains no "one-way", no "30 km/h" and no loop for traffic [S-B04 SMEC, full text searched]. It only suggests a "review of speed limits" and "lower speeds or shared traffic zones" in the CBD [S-B04 SMEC p.71], plus wayfinding and dynamic parking signage [S-B04 SMEC pp.62-63].
  - The loop and the 30 km/h limit appear for the first time on the SMEC/Council "Vision" and "Town Centre" maps tabled 26 October 2026 [S-B03 pp.92-93].
  - The 2017 Tonkin town-centre strategy proposed neither [S-B22]. The 2014 Mainstreet Precinct Master Plan proposed a "shared space" on Ocean Street and a new one-way road west of the railway creating "circulation loops" with McKinlay Street [S-B26 pp.9, 13].
- **Where the outer Ring Road came from.** The existing "Ring Road" (Welch Road and Armstrong Road) was built by Council in 2002 [S-B29 p.214]. Until mid-2026 every council document treats it as the protected high-speed South Coast Freight Route: the 2024 B-double gazettal [S-B30], UGMS Objective 3.7 [S-B10 p.52] and the January 2026 working-group briefing [S-B31].
  - SMEC's August 2026 report says Armstrong Road "is the preferred freight route" [S-B04 SMEC p.50]. It adds only that an alternative bypass further north "should be explored" [S-B04 SMEC pp.66, 74-75].
  - The October 2026 maps then show an "Outer (Freight) Ring Road (indicative)" in the "Vision", and present retaining the current road as the alternative [S-B03 pp.91-92, 94].
  - No route study, cost estimate or DIT position on an outer ring road was found.
- **Rigor.** The evidence base is thin for the specific town-centre and ring-road choices:
  - The model (VHRAM) is a "simplified" weekday sketch model with fixed mode shares, base year 2021 and one GARP-high "ultimate" scenario. Only a "Do Nothing" network was tested, with no alternative growth scenarios [S-B04 SMEC pp.43-45, 49, 77-78].
  - SMEC states there is "no count information on the main east-west road corridor outside of the extremities". It also states there was "not sufficient time" to review network or land-use assumptions with Council [S-B04 SMEC pp.72, 78].
  - The only seasonal data used is one permanent counter on Victor Harbor Road (2025) [S-B04 SMEC p.41]. Parking data dates from surveys in 2015 [S-B04 SMEC p.37].
  - No intersection (SIDRA) or microsimulation modelling, no peer review and no testing of the one-way loop, the 30 km/h limit or the outer ring road are documented. SMEC describes testing the "Ultimate Vision" as future work for February to June 2027 [S-B03 p.78].

### E5: Modelling

- **Model type.** VHRAM (Victor Harbor Roads Assessment Model) is "a simplified transport demand model". It covers trip generation, distribution and assignment, with mode choice fixed as percentages, for a typical weekday [S-B04 SMEC pp.43-45].
- **Area and years.** The model covers Victor Harbor, Port Elliot, Middleton, Goolwa and Hindmarsh Island [S-B04 SMEC p.44]. The base year is 2021. The future is a single "ultimate" GARP High scenario with no specific year: population 35,300 in the Victor Harbor SA2 and 58,900 across the model area [S-B04 SMEC p.49].
- **Network tested.** Only a "Do Nothing" network was tested. No new Hindmarsh River crossing was included, and the only change was Waggon Road realigned as a four-way intersection [S-B04 SMEC pp.52, 78].
- **Calibration.** The report says the tool "has been developed and calibrated specifically for Victor Harbor" [S-B04 SMEC p.78]. No calibration statistics, count sites, screenlines or validation criteria are published.
- **Limitations stated by SMEC itself** [S-B04 SMEC pp.77-78]:
  - "no testing of alternative growth scenarios has been completed";
  - count gaps limit certainty on key links;
  - there was "not sufficient time" to review with Council the road network, public-transport uplift and land-use assumptions.
- **Town-centre findings.** Torrens Street and Hindmarsh Road would be "nearing capacity but not exceeding" in the ultimate scenario [S-B04 SMEC p.54]. "Signalisation of intersections" is suggested, mainly as safer pedestrian crossing points [S-B04 SMEC p.50]. Signalising roundabouts "will be required as key intersections ... reach capacity" [S-B04 SMEC p.71].
- **What was not modelled:**
  - No SIDRA, intersection-level or microsimulation modelling is mentioned in the SMEC report (searched: "SIDRA", "microsim", "VISSIM", "Aimsun": 0 hits) [S-B04].
  - The one-way loop, the 30 km/h limit and the outer ring road do not appear in the model scenarios [S-B04].
  - The October report schedules "Ultimate Vision testing, coding, modelling" for February-June 2027, after consultation [S-B03 p.78].
- **Earlier modelling.** The 2017 Tonkin work used "preliminary traffic modelling (using industry standard SIDRA software)" for Grantley Avenue closure options, peak times of "a typical day" [S-B22 p.7]. For Hindmarsh Road / Seaview Road / Eyre Terrace it said "further detailed investigations and modelling" were needed before signals could be judged viable [S-B22 p.14].

### E6: Parking surveys

- **2015-16 surveys.** On-street parking surveys were done on 2 December 2015, 30 December 2015 and 13 February 2016 [S-B22 p.4]. SMEC restates the results: about 2,000 spaces (513 on-street), 26-29% occupancy on typical weekdays in December 2015 and 29-45% between Christmas and New Year, peaking 11:00-13:30 [S-B04 SMEC p.37].
- **Reused in 2026.** SMEC assumes "similar patterns" still apply because no major policy changes were made [S-B04 SMEC p.37]. It also recommends new itemised parking and turnover data, including seasonality [S-B04 SMEC p.72].
- **Inference:** the "digital parking signage" element rests on parking data that is about 11 years old, plus a stated "perception issue" about supply [S-B04 SMEC p.70].

### E10: Options considered and rejected

- **In VHITS: no options appraisal is documented** for the town-centre elements. There is no comparison of a one-way loop against two-way operation, of 30 km/h against 40 km/h, or of signals against roundabouts.
  - The October report presents the maps as "designed to initiate engagement and discussion (for consultation purpose only)" [S-B03 p.77].
- **Ring Road.** Two alternatives are mapped [S-B03 pp.77, 91, 94; S-B02 p.5]:
  - **"Existing Freight Bypass":** keep the high-speed Ring Road and build a second town centre north of it.
  - **"Alternative Freight Bypass":** the Ring Road becomes a 60 km/h collector or distributor, and a new outer freight route is built.
  - However, the "Vision" map already shows the "Outer (Freight) Ring Road (indicative)" [S-B03 p.92].
  - SMEC's Phase 1 text had pointed the other way. It names Armstrong Road the preferred freight route [S-B04 SMEC p.50], lists ways to manage the Waggon Road crossing (including relocating the freight route north) [S-B04 SMEC p.73-74], and calls for a regional freight strategy to explore a northern bypass [S-B04 SMEC pp.74-75]. No reasons for the shift are documented.
- **Rejected by Council at the motion stage.** The administration's alternative wording (cost UGMS actions first, then do a Traffic Strategy later) was not adopted; Council instead ordered work to start in 2025/26 [S-B06 p.318; S-B07 p.15].
- **Earlier rejections, 2017** [S-B22 pp.6-7, 12-13]:
  - right-turn ban into Ocean Street (Mainstreet Plan 2014) discounted for its access impact;
  - Stuart Street closure and bus stop relocation discounted for bus operations;
  - Torrens St / Victoria St signals not supported;
  - full Grantley Avenue closure replaced by a left-in/left-out trial.
- **Freight corridor via Inman Valley Road** was described as "not possible" because of the investment required; a Middleton bypass is preferred [S-B31 item 5.1].

### Rigor assessment (direct answers)

- **Were counts taken in school holidays, or for one week in winter?** No VHITS-commissioned counts are documented. SMEC used estimated AADT plus one permanent counter's 2025 daily series, which does span holiday peaks [S-B04 SMEC pp.21-22, 41]. The 2017 turning counts were taken in April 2017 [S-B22 p.9]. ## 4. Discoveries

- **Phasing compressed in time.** The tender was advertised 24 January 2026 and the project initiated in April 2026. Phase 1 was due 30 June 2026, inside the same financial year [S-B21; S-B03 p.77; S-B04 SMEC p.4].
- **The Phase 1 report and the consultation map disagree on freight.** The report prefers Armstrong Road as the freight route; the "Vision" map shows an outer freight ring road [S-B04 SMEC p.50; S-B03 p.92].
- **The Phase 1 report shows no one-way loop.** The loop and the 30 km/h limit first appear in October 2026 maps prepared after a 30 September staff workshop [S-B03 pp.79, 92-93].
- **The same officers appear across a decade.** David Read and Graham Pathuis were on the 2014 Mainstreet Precinct Master Plan core team [S-B26 p.1]. David Read authored the 2025 UGMS report, the 2025 motion commentary and the 2026 VHITS report [S-B08; S-B06; S-B03]. Graham Pathuis was Director Planning in 2017 [S-B25]. URPS worked on 2014, 2017 and 2026 [S-B26; S-B22; S-B04].
- **Mayor Moira Jenkins moved both 2017 resolutions** on the town-centre strategy as a councillor [S-B24; S-B25].
- **A disability committee pushed for a seat at the table.** The DAIAC asked to be consulted directly on the ITS, and Council agreed on 22 June 2026 [S-B15].
- **AMP2 still carries 2017-strategy projects** to 2029/30 (Burke Street / Crozier Road, Oval Road / Kingsford Street, Carlyle Street) [S-B18 p.35].
- **Officer titles are inconsistent.** The 26 October report lists "Interim Chief Executive Officer - Graham Pathuis" and "Acting Director Community and Development - Jodi Roberts" as consulted [S-B03 p.79]. The same night's minutes list CEO Chris White, Director Community and Development Graham Pathuis and Director Environment and Infrastructure Jodi Roberts [S-B05 p.2]. The 12 October briefing notice was signed by Acting CEO Karen Rokicinski [S-B13].
- **Council has joined freight-bypass advocacy.** It joined the City of Burnside's High Productivity Vehicle Network / Greater Adelaide Freight Bypass campaign in October 2025 [S-B37].
