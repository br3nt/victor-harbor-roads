Recommendations

Draft — recommendations to the City of Victor Harbor arising from the investigation, to be updated with simulation results. Each links to its evidence.

1. Test before consulting, and before endorsing a "Vision"

The council's timeline puts "testing, coding, modelling, staging priorities … costings" in February–June 2027 — after the public is asked to rate the options and after Council endorses an "Ultimate Vision" in February 2027 [S-B03 p.78]. The order should be reversed: model each option (including summer peak and event days), publish the results, then consult. See Due Diligence Assessment.

2. Trial before committing, wherever possible

Changes such as a one-way loop, lower speed limits or shared zones can be trialled cheaply with temporary infrastructure (signs, bollards, plastic kerbing, temporary line marking) before money is spent on permanent works. A trial should:

  • Run through known busy times — the summer school holidays, Christmas–New Year, Easter and at least one major event — not only a quiet month (Seasons and Events).
  • Have clear "before" truth data collected under the same conditions in advance: turning counts at the affected intersections, travel times on the main routes, queue lengths, parking occupancy, crashes and near-misses.
  • Collect the same "after" data during the trial, with success criteria agreed and published beforehand (for example: travel time from Encounter Bay and McCracken to the town centre no worse than X minutes at the summer peak; no queue back across the rail crossing).
  • Publish both datasets and the comparison before any permanent decision.

Council has done this before on a small scale: the Grantley Avenue left-in/left-out trial from the 2017 strategy, run with the Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) [S-B41].

3. Collect current data first

The newest detailed town-centre counts are from April 2017, during school holidays [S-D19]; the parking data is from 2015–16; SMEC (the consultancy) took no new counts and says count gaps limit certainty [S-B04 pp.72, 77–78]. Before deciding: term-time and summer turning counts at the town-centre intersections, permanent counters on Torrens St and Hindmarsh Rd, and travel-time surveys.

4. Resolve the traffic-volume discrepancy

SMEC's report lists Hindmarsh Rd at 9,500 and Torrens St at 8,500 vehicles a day; the Department for Infrastructure and Transport's (DIT) published Annual Average Daily Traffic is 17,300–19,300 and 12,900–15,500 [S-B04 p.22] [S-D01]. Council should confirm which figures its model uses.

5. Test both growth scenarios

The council's own Urban Growth Management Strategy (UGMS) plans for about +8,000 people by 2050 on trend, or more than +19,200 under the State's high-growth targets; building since 2021 is below even the trend rate [S-B10 pp.13, 20, 41]. Options should work under both, with infrastructure staged to actual growth.

6. Publish the model and the data; get it peer reviewed

No calibration statistics, count sites or peer review are published for the Victor Harbor Roads Assessment Model (VHRAM, SMEC's model) [S-B04 p.78]. Publishing them lets residents and independent reviewers check the basis of decisions.

7. Put alternatives on the table

The survey offered no alternatives for the town centre. Options to test alongside the council's: roundabout slip lanes, a two-lane entry roundabout at McCracken, widening the existing ring road, and time- or event-based speed limits on variable signs (Options Register).

8. Settle the Department for Infrastructure and Transport's (DIT) position early

Torrens St and Hindmarsh Rd are Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) roads and primary freight routes; DIT must approve all speed-limit changes, even on council roads [S-M04 s4.1], and decides on signals there [Approvals and Constraints]. Its guideline allows 30 km/h or 40 km/h in town centres, but says freight routes and major traffic routes are unsuitable for the high-pedestrian-activity limit, and that the limit is not intended for streets with short holiday or tourism peaks [S-M04 s5.7.1]. Its position should be known before options are put to the public.
