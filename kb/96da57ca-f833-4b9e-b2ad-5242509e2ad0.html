People and Roles

Who shaped the proposals, and how (R28). Every role claim cites a record. Only public-office and professional roles are tracked; members of the public appear only where they spoke publicly (deputations, petitions, the public post that started this) — no motives are inferred. Once the meeting records land (stream B), each significant person gets their own note, and decisions link to them with typed links (rel="moved-by", rel="seconded-by", rel="authored-by", …) so the graph answers "who did what" in both directions.

Role vocabulary

Decision-maker
Voted on or resolved a decision (councillor, committee member).
Approver
Holds a statutory or delegated approval (Department for Infrastructure and Transport [DIT], the Minister, the Chief Executive Officer [CEO] or a delegated officer).
Originator
First raised an idea on the record.
Author / worker
Prepared the reports, data or designs (officers, consultants).
Mover / seconder
Moved or seconded a motion.
Supporter / Opposer
Spoke or voted for / against, on the record.
Passed without debate
Decision recorded en bloc or without discussion — a fact about the record, not a judgement of the people.
Abstained / conflict declared / absent
As recorded in minutes.
Public voice
Deputation, petition lead, or public post/comment in the debate.

Votes and debate on the record

  • No division was called on any VHITS-related resolution, so no individual votes are recorded; "decided-by" links mean present and entitled to vote [S-B07] [S-B15] [S-B05].
  • The Dec 2025 motion was carried unanimously; the Oct 2026 consultation approval was recorded as "CARRIED" (some other items that night were recorded "CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY") with no amendment. A division was called on one other item that night, item 9.4 [S-B05 pp.6, 8-9]. Councillors had three open briefings on the strategy beforehand (11 May, 15 Jul, 12 Oct 2026) [S-B11] [S-B12] [S-B13]. The minutes' format does not record debate; the meeting video does (next item).
  • Item 10.1 was debated, for about five minutes (1:06:50 to 1:12:04 in the council's video), according to YouTube's auto-generated captions, which may contain errors; check the video before quoting [S-M05]. Mayor Jenkins invited the mover (Cr Kemp) and seconder (Cr Mann) to speak. Speakers, in order, from the captions:
    • Cr Mann: done a few workshops; visionary strategy; take it to public consultation and get feedback [S-M05 1:07:20].
    • Cr Schiller: by consulting on a strategy, the council can reach trigger points for issues like intersections and decide on a strategy, not ad hoc [S-M05].
    • Cr Burns: asked that cost estimates be included in the consultation material, because the cost depends on State or Federal funding and the public might think the council will pay [S-M05]. The fact sheet released for consultation has no cost estimates [S-A03].
    • Mayor Jenkins: agreed it was a good point; 50-year strategy, very aspirational and council can't afford that on its own [S-M05].
    • Cr Schofield: we can't get our hopes up; hoped some things could happen sooner and cheaper; said the plan covers a broader area than the City [S-M05].
    • Cr Kemp, closing: 50-year visionary strategy; looking for short, medium and long-term priorities and to see what the community says first [S-M05 1:11:25].
    The Mayor then put the motion and said that's carried [S-M05 1:12:04]. The captions show no speaker opposing it and no division was called; they cannot show who voted which way.
  • Observation (ours): none of the six speakers mentioned the one-way loop, 30 km/h limits, the signals, the outer ring road or modelling evidence [S-M05].
  • No conflicts of interest were declared on Victor Harbor Integrated Transport Strategy (VHITS) items [S-B05] [S-B07] [S-B15].
  • Continuity: David Read, Graham Pathuis and URPS all worked on the 2014 Mainstreet plan, which had the earliest loop-like "circulation loops" idea [S-B26]. That is a fact about continuity, not evidence of who proposed the 2026 loop.
