Simulation Results

Preliminary. First results from our SUMO (Simulation of Urban MObility) model of Victor Harbor now (traffic built from 2025 data): term weekday and summer peak day, 3 runs (random seeds) each. Independently reviewed on 21 Nov 2026; wording below follows that review. Method: Simulation Design; options: Options Register; assumptions and calibration fit: sim/assumptions/integration.md (A-INT-01 to 04) and sim/assumptions/S6-scenarios.md.

How to read these results

  • Differences, not forecasts. The model is partly calibrated: most main roads are within about ±25% of Department for Infrastructure and Transport (DIT) daily counts, but Victoria St carries about 50% too much and Adelaide Rd about 40% too little, and the town centre is somewhat under-loaded: at the counted intersections the model carries about 0.80 of the 2017 turning counts projected to 2025 (method and validation: sim/data/counts/PROJECTION.md; projected counts). Delay grows faster than traffic, so penalties in the town centre are more likely understated than overstated.
  • Noise. The 3 seeds share the same trips, so the spread shown is simulation noise only, not uncertainty about demand.
  • Metrics. "Trip time" is door-to-door driving time. "Time stopped" is total vehicle-hours stationary in queues; it does not depend on speed limits (unlike the simulator's "time loss" measure, which we do not use). Part of the extra trip time in lower-speed options is the lower limits themselves, assuming full compliance.
  • As modelled. Signals (C4) are a minimal design (one shared lane per approach, equal green split) and probably pessimistic; 20 km/h shared streets (C2) have no pedestrian delay and are probably optimistic. Brent's package here is B3+B4+B5 (time-based speed zones, B2, not included).

Better options: aimed at where the delay actually is (R16)

We first located the delay on today's network (method, hotspot tables and assumptions: sim/assumptions/S7-improvements.md), then designed options aimed at it and ran them like the others. Most delay is side-street right turns onto one-lane main roads around midday (10:00–17:00 holds 83–89% of it), not the town-centre roundabouts (most approaches average 3–8 s). The largest hotspots are Victoria St between Bay Rd and Torrens St (car-park and side-street turns), side streets onto Hindmarsh Rd, the Bridge Tce / Grantley Ave link onto Hindmarsh Rd, the Seaview/Eyre roundabout, and, as the town grows, Crozier Rd and Greenhills Rd joining Armstrong Rd (Crozier Rd drivers wait 53–74 s a vehicle at 2041 high growth, about 15 years from now).

  • Today's traffic: N9 (Crozier Rd turn lanes at Armstrong Rd plus a roundabout at Hindmarsh Rd / Grantley Ave) is the best tested: about 11% less time stopped on a term weekday and 18% less on a summer day (the summer range across seeds, 364–386 vehicle-hours, is clear of today's 450–472).
  • As the town grows: N7 (roundabouts at Armstrong/Crozier, Armstrong/Greenhills and Hindmarsh/Grantley) is the best tested at 2041 (about 15 years from now) high growth (about −24%), while both the council's package and Brent's are about +60%. A staged reading: N9 now, then the Armstrong Rd roundabouts as the growth areas fill.
  • Growth is still not solved: even N7 leaves 2041 weekday queueing at about 3.4 times today's, and no tested option fixes the Victoria St corridor or the Hindmarsh Rd side streets (about 80% of summer delay stays where it is under N9).
  • Didn't help: N6 (turn bans: traffic moves onto other side streets; +22% in summer) and N8 (Victoria St left-turn lanes). The council's outer freight ring road (C5) was not built: its indicative map could not be placed reliably.

Findings (preliminary, as modelled)

  • Council's town-centre package: about +28 s per trip and about +60% time stopped in queues on a summer peak day (+275 vehicle-hours a day); about +14 s per trip on a term weekday. Part of the extra trip time is the lower limits themselves.
  • Which part matters: on a summer peak day the one-way loop is the largest of the components modelled separately (time stopped +44%; anticlockwise worse); on a term weekday the signals are (+23%) and the loop is neutral.
  • Foreshore: the clockwise loop sends about 180 trucks a day (about 10–15 today), twice the caravans in summer, and three to five times the cars via Bridge Terrace and the north end of Flinders Parade into Coral St, while the Esplanade itself carries fewer cars (one-way). Total car driving on the foreshore changes little.
  • Side streets: under the council package Acraman St carries about three times today's traffic, and Lindsay St residents wait about 80 s instead of 15 s to get out onto Crozier Rd. Acraman St already has crash hotspots (crash data).
  • Everyday trips: in summer, Encounter Bay to a supermarket is about a minute longer on a typical trip and about 2 minutes on the slowest; McCracken to medical services typically 1–1.5 min longer, the slowest 2–4 min.
  • Brent's package: neutral network-wide at today's demand. The four-lane ring road as modelled leaves Crozier Rd / Armstrong Rd a give-way, and drivers from Crozier Rd then wait about 50–55 s instead of about 29 s — the junction would need upgrading with the widening.

Known issues to check

  • Under the council package, Torrens Lane (tagged a residential street in OpenStreetMap) goes from tens to 500–700 vehicles a day. If it is really a narrow service lane, this inflates the package's penalty slightly.
  • Growth: at 2041 (about 15 years from now) high growth (State high series) a term weekday finishes (see better options), but a summer day does not: every option jams by early afternoon, and at 2051 (about 25 years from now) high growth even a term weekday jams in every option tested (sim/assumptions/integration.md A-INT-05). Growth areas load onto today's roads, without their future streets and link roads. Saturday, New Year's Eve and the outer ring road options (C5/C6) are not yet run.
